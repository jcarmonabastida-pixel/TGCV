# TGCV — Research Prospectus v0.2 (Post-EMP-1.1)

**Status:** Internal canonical draft — post-empirical update
**Version relation:** supersedes the evidential state of v0.1; does not erase prior versions.

## 1. Research programme

TGCV investigates whether a transversal analytical layer can explain how interactions modify the set of transformations accessible to a system, how those modifications shape subsequent trajectories, and under what conditions they can be related to value creation.

The programme is broader than the theory currently being developed. TGCV is its first major theoretical result, not the definition of the programme itself.

## 2. Central research object

The central object remains **interactions that modify the set of transformations accessible to a system**.

The programme therefore studies a sequence of the form:

`S → T_acc = F(S,C,L) → ΔT_acc → ΔTrajectory → Value`

where value is treated as a downstream consequence to be investigated rather than assumed as an intrinsic property of accessibility.

## 3. Scientific problem

Existing research addresses fragments of this phenomenon through adaptive systems, dynamic adaptation, affordances, dynamic capabilities, self-* systems, runtime composition, learning, evolution and related traditions. The programme investigates whether these fragments can be connected through a minimal representation that remains meaningful across domains.

This is framed as a falsifiable research programme, not as a claim that such a universal representation has already been established.

## 4. Current empirical evidence

TGCV-EMP-1.1 provides the programme's first completed confirmatory computational evidence under its sealed operationalisation.

The primary result was a mean test LogLoss improvement of `+0.07942` for the TGCV condition against the baseline, above the predeclared `0.04` threshold, with paired sign-flip Monte-Carlo `p < 0.000005`. A count-only accessibility control improved by `+0.01809`, while permutation of the relational accessibility representation produced `-0.09496`. A structural intervention used 5,000 matched pairs while preserving conventional summaries and altering relational structure.

## 5. Interpretation of the evidence

The result provides positive evidence that the specified computational representation of accessibility contains predictive information beyond the tested baseline and beyond accessibility cardinality alone.

It does **not** establish universal validity of TGCV, causal efficacy across domains, or validity of every future operationalisation.

The appropriate epistemic status is therefore:

**empirically supported, theoretically provisional, replication required.**

## 6. Research questions after EMP-1.1

### RQ1 — Ontological
Can the phenomenon of interaction-induced modification of accessible transformations be represented through a minimal domain-independent structure without collapsing into existing constructs?

### RQ2 — Explanatory
Under what conditions does a change in accessibility predict a change in subsequent system trajectories?

### RQ3 — Empirical
Does the accessibility representation retain predictive or explanatory value under independently specified operationalisations and external datasets/domains?

### RQ4 — Boundary conditions
Where does the TGCV representation fail, become non-identifiable, or add no explanatory value beyond conventional state/resource representations?

### RQ5 — Value
Under what conditions, if any, can changes in accessible transformations be related systematically to the creation of practical value?

## 7. Immediate research programme

The next phase is not retrospective optimisation of EMP-1.1. It consists of:

1. preserve the sealed EMP-1.1 result;
2. consolidate the theoretical and ontological consequences without post-hoc alteration of the Core;
3. design an independent replication operationalisation;
4. test TGCV against external empirical material where feasible;
5. examine boundary conditions and negative cases;
6. progressively derive the TGCV methodology and reference architecture from validated knowledge rather than assuming them in advance;
7. connect validated scientific results to practical transfer and value construction.

## 8. Falsifiability and negative results

The programme remains falsifiable. Replication failure, null effects under appropriate controls, or systematic boundary conditions that invalidate the proposed explanatory relation are legitimate scientific outcomes. They must not be treated as implementation defects unless independently demonstrated to be so.

## 9. Contribution architecture

The expected contribution is layered:

- a scientific account of the phenomenon;
- an ontology and formal model where justified by evidence;
- a reference architecture and methodology derived from validated principles;
- empirical demonstrations and boundary conditions;
- a pathway for practical application and transfer.

## 10. Current maturity

TGCV has moved beyond a purely conceptual hypothesis because a confirmatory computational test has produced positive evidence. It has not yet reached general empirical validation.

The principal scientific objective now is therefore **independent replication and generalisation**, while preserving theoretical discipline and avoiding confirmation-driven expansion of the Core.

## 11. Programme-level principle

The programme is organised around a flow of knowledge rather than around documents:

`Problem → Questions → Evidence → Knowledge → Theory → Design → Impact → Programme Learning`

The assets exist to stabilise and govern this flow. The methodology is expected to become reproducible through programme learning; it is not assumed to be complete in advance.
