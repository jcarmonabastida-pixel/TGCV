# TGCV — Methodology Post-EMP-1.1 v0.2

**Status:** Provisional scientific methodology — NOT FROZEN

## 1. Purpose

Define the current reproducible workflow for investigating accessibility-changing interactions while preserving the distinction between programme methodology, TGCV theory and any single experimental implementation.

The methodology is a product of the Programme's accumulated learning. It is not assumed to be complete or universally valid.

## 2. Governing principle

**Evidence precedes theory; theory precedes methodology; methodology precedes transfer.**

Operationally, the Programme follows:

`Problem → Questions → Search → Evidence Bank → Case Files → Fact Bank → Synthesis → Ontological Model → Theory → Operationalisation → Validation → Architecture/Method → Transfer → Programme Learning`.

## 3. Phase I — Discovery

### 3.1 Problem formulation
Define the phenomenon, scope and uncertainty before selecting supporting evidence.

### 3.2 Question formulation
Translate the problem into explicit research questions and falsifiable claims.

### 3.3 Search
Conduct systematic and targeted evidence retrieval, recording provenance and selection decisions.

### 3.4 Evidence Bank
Store source-level evidence with provenance, scope and reliability information.

### 3.5 Case Files
Organise evidence into structured cases or phenomena without prematurely forcing theoretical categories.

### 3.6 Fact Bank
Separate observed or source-supported facts from interpretations and hypotheses.

## 4. Phase II — Explanation

### 4.1 Synthesis
Identify recurring structures, divergences, gaps and candidate relations.

### 4.2 Ontological modelling
Construct a provisional ontology only where synthesis warrants it. Preserve competing interpretations where evidence does not discriminate.

### 4.3 Theory construction
Formulate the minimal theoretical relation and explicit falsifiable consequences.

For TGCV, the current core relation is:

`S → T_acc = F(S,C,L) → ΔT_acc → ΔTrajectory → Value`.

## 5. Phase III — Operationalisation

Every empirical claim must be translated into a pre-specified operationalisation.

At minimum this requires:

- declared system representation;
- declared context and constraints/resources;
- declared transformation universe;
- accessibility predicates evaluable without outcome leakage;
- outcome variables;
- controls and null models;
- statistical/evaluation procedure;
- provenance and reproducibility anchors.

Operationalisation is an instance of the theory, not a replacement for it.

## 6. Phase IV — Validation

Validation must be capable of producing negative results.

A valid protocol therefore specifies before outcome inspection:

- primary hypothesis;
- primary metric;
- threshold or decision rule;
- controls;
- exclusion rules;
- analysis procedure;
- computational environment where relevant.

Post-hoc changes require a new version and cannot be represented as part of the original confirmatory test.

## 7. Phase V — Architecture and methodology

Only after sufficient empirical support should reusable architectural or methodological principles be stabilised.

EMP-1.1 supports the accessibility representation used in that experiment, but does not by itself establish a universal TGCV methodology.

The current methodology is therefore explicitly provisional.

## 8. Phase VI — Transfer

Validated principles may feed:

- reference architectures;
- practical methods;
- pilots;
- industrial collaborations;
- exploitation and impact planning.

Transfer cannot be used to upgrade the evidential status of scientific claims.

## 9. Phase VII — Programme learning

Every completed research cycle is evaluated for lessons about the research process itself.

Learning may modify:

- search protocols;
- evidence management;
- synthesis procedures;
- operationalisation practices;
- validation gates;
- governance mechanisms;
- future methodological guidance.

This is the fourth cycle of the Programme: **Learning**.

## 10. Methodological invariants

1. Preserve provenance.
2. Separate evidence, facts, interpretations and hypotheses.
3. Declare empirical tests before observing outcomes.
4. Do not alter theory retrospectively to fit successful results.
5. Preserve negative evidence and failed hypotheses.
6. Keep scientific claims separate from transfer claims.
7. Treat methods as revisable outputs of programme learning.

## 11. Current status after EMP-1.1

The methodology has been sufficiently specified to guide independent replication, but it has not yet been validated as a domain-independent research methodology.

**Status:** operationally usable; scientifically provisional; subject to replication and programme learning.
