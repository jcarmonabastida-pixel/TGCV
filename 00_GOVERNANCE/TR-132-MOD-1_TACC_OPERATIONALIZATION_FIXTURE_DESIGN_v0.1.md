# TR-132-MOD-1 — Methodological T_acc Operationalization Fixture Design v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Status:** CURRENT / OPERATIVE — DESIGN-ONLY  
**Execution:** NOT AUTHORIZED  
**Origin:** TR-132-G0  
**Scientific result:** NONE

## 1. Purpose

Define a controlled methodological fixture in which `T_acc` can be independently specified and adjudicated without relying on a real-domain event trace, dataset outcome, industrial partner evidence, or downstream performance.

The fixture is a methodological instrument for testing operational identifiability. It is not evidence that any real-world domain satisfies the same accessibility conditions.

## 2. Design question

Can a finite transformation environment be constructed such that, before any transformation is realized, the following are independently frozen and auditable:

- system state/context `S,C`;
- applicable constraints `L`;
- candidate transformation universe `T`;
- stable transformation identity;
- material/setup/authorization conditions;
- accessibility predicate;
- evidence sufficiency rules;
- distinction between accessible and realized transformations?

If yes, the fixture can be used to test L1, L2 and potentially L3 identifiability. If not, the result is methodological INCONCLUSIVE/FAIL according to the TR-132 decision rules; it does not imply that real-world `T_acc` is impossible to operationalize.

## 3. Fixture architecture

The fixture shall contain four explicitly separated layers:

1. **State layer:** a finite, frozen representation of system/context conditions.
2. **Transformation layer:** a finite, explicitly enumerated universe `T = {τ1,...,τn}` with stable identities.
3. **Accessibility layer:** independently declared conditions determining whether each `τ` is accessible at the decision point.
4. **Realization layer:** an independently controlled record of which accessible or inaccessible candidates are actually realized.

The realization layer MUST NOT determine the accessibility layer.

## 4. Required construction properties

The fixture MUST satisfy:

### 4.1 Finite completeness
The candidate universe is finite and exhaustively enumerated by construction. No post-hoc candidate can be added because it produces a favorable result.

### 4.2 Ex-ante accessibility
Accessibility status for each candidate is determined from frozen fixture state and frozen conditions before realization is inspected.

### 4.3 Independent realization
The fixture permits realization to be varied independently of accessibility, including cases where an accessible transformation is not realized.

### 4.4 Stable identity
Each transformation has a persistent identifier and explicit preconditions/postconditions sufficient for matching across timepoints.

### 4.5 Controlled state transition
If a transformation is realized, the resulting state is generated by a frozen transition rule. Realization is therefore downstream from accessibility adjudication.

### 4.6 Auditability
Every accessibility decision has a machine- or table-auditable basis identifying the relevant state, conditions, rule and evidence class.

## 5. Formal objects

The fixture shall instantiate:

`T_poss(S,C,L)` — candidates technically possible within the fixture.

`T_adm(S,C,L)` — candidates satisfying the fixture's explicitly declared admissibility rules.

`T_acc(S,C,L,E)` — candidates satisfying the frozen accessibility predicate using only pre-realization information `E`.

`T_obs` — candidates actually realized during a run.

The fixture MUST permit all four sets to differ. In particular, at least one test configuration should satisfy:

`τ ∈ T_acc` and `τ ∉ T_obs`.

This is a mandatory control against the invalid inference `T_obs = T_acc`.

## 6. Accessibility predicate

The predicate shall be specified independently of any expected result. A candidate is certified accessible only if all mandatory fixture conditions are satisfied, for example:

`ACCESSIBLE(τ,t) = IDENTITY ∧ STATE_MATCH ∧ ADMISSIBLE ∧ MATERIALS_AVAILABLE ∧ SETUP_AVAILABLE ∧ AUTHORIZATION_AVAILABLE ∧ TIME_WINDOW_VALID ∧ EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT`

The exact predicate instance must be frozen in a later executable package. The illustrative conjunction above is a design template, not an executed criterion.

Any unknown, ambiguous or conflicting mandatory condition shall prevent certification unless the frozen rule explicitly defines another auditable disposition. It must never be converted to accessible because the transformation was later realized.

## 7. L-level progression

### L1
Demonstrate at least one `τ` whose accessibility is certified independently of realization.

### L2
Demonstrate a pre-specified bounded subset `T_acc+` whose membership is independently certified for all included candidates under the frozen rules.

### L3
Create two frozen fixture states `t0` and `t1` with the same candidate universe, identity rules and accessibility predicate, while changing only pre-specified state/condition variables. Demonstrate:

`T_acc,t0+ ≠ T_acc,t1+`.

The difference must be attributable to the frozen state/condition change, not to altered candidate selection or evidence thresholds.

### L4
Not required for this methodological fixture. Full-space closure is achieved by construction only if the fixture's complete accessibility universe and all material conditions are demonstrably exhaustive.

## 8. Anti-circularity experiment controls

Before any realization run, freeze:

- fixture version;
- candidate universe;
- transformation identities;
- state configurations;
- accessibility predicate;
- evidence rules;
- subset rule;
- temporal comparison rule, if L3;
- realization schedule/control;
- result schema.

The run shall include an **accessibility freeze point** after which these definitions cannot be changed without invalidating the run.

The adjudicator may inspect accessibility evidence before realization, but the accessibility decision must be recorded/frozen before realization outcomes are disclosed to the adjudication process.

## 9. Negative controls

The fixture should contain, where feasible:

1. an accessible-but-unrealized candidate;
2. an inaccessible candidate that is not realized;
3. a candidate technically possible but inadmissible;
4. a candidate admissible but inaccessible because a frozen material/setup condition is absent;
5. an evidence-indeterminate candidate.

These controls test whether the operationalization preserves the distinctions among `T_poss`, `T_adm`, `T_acc` and `T_obs`.

## 10. Independence from downstream constructs

No Reach, Trajectory, Outcome or Value variable is required to determine fixture accessibility. Such variables may be generated later as separate downstream objects, but they MUST NOT enter the accessibility predicate or candidate-subset selection.

Therefore the fixture can test the accessibility construct without presupposing the downstream TGCV chain.

## 11. Methodological validity criteria

The fixture design passes design audit only if all are true:

- candidate universe is finite and frozen;
- identity is stable;
- accessibility conditions are independently specified;
- accessibility is decidable from pre-realization fixture information;
- realization can differ from accessibility;
- missing evidence has an explicit disposition;
- L1/L2/L3 criteria are mechanically or procedurally auditable;
- no downstream outcome enters accessibility definition;
- no criterion can be relaxed after observing realization;
- fixture status remains methodological and does not create real-domain evidence.

## 12. What the fixture can and cannot establish

A successful fixture can establish that the current analytical construct admits a non-circular operational representation under a controlled environment. It may support a methodological BOUNDED PASS for TR-132 if the resulting execution satisfies the governed protocol.

It cannot, by itself, establish that real-world Rust, industrial systems, or arbitrary domains possess independently observable accessibility conditions of the same form. Transfer to a real domain requires a separate admission and evidence path.

A failed fixture may indicate inadequacy of the proposed operationalization or of the fixture design. It does not by itself falsify the TGCV Core.
