# D-OPS-24 — Directed Documentary Accessibility Search Protocol v0.1

**Status:** CURRENT / OPERATIVE — DESIGN-ONLY  
**Execution:** NOT AUTHORIZED  
**Origin:** Post-IT-G1 industrial discovery boundary  
**Scientific impact:** NO SCIENTIFIC CLAIM CHANGE

## 1. Purpose

Define a bounded documentary search for domains in which the admissible/accessibility conditions of candidate transformations may be specified independently of realized event traces.

The purpose is **not** to find a favorable industrial case, validate O3, demonstrate utility, or rescue the Industrial Track. The purpose is to test whether the present accessibility-identifiability boundary can be overcome by a different evidence architecture.

## 2. Research question

Can a real-world operational domain provide, independently of observed realization, sufficient documentary evidence to reconstruct at a decision time:

`(S_t, C_t) → candidate transformation → accessibility/admissibility conditions → (S_{t+1}, C_{t+1})`?

## 3. Inclusion criterion

A domain is eligible only if documentary sources jointly permit identification of:

1. a bounded system and unit of analysis;
2. a temporal frame;
3. candidate transformations/actions;
4. ex-ante admissibility/accessibility conditions for those candidates;
5. material, setup, temporal or authorization constraints where relevant;
6. evidence sufficient to distinguish an unobserved candidate from an inaccessible candidate;
7. sufficient state/context information to reconstruct the relevant decision point.

A domain may be retained for further review only if the accessibility condition is independently grounded rather than inferred from realized events.

## 4. Search families

Prioritize documentary architectures such as formal operating procedures with explicit alternatives and eligibility rules; regulatory or compliance decision procedures with published admissibility rules; safety-critical operational procedures with explicit action envelopes; workflow systems whose admissible transitions are specified independently of execution logs; controlled operational protocols with versioned rules and conditions; and other domains where the normative/operational specification predates or is independent of observed outcomes.

Public event logs alone are insufficient unless an independent specification closes accessibility.

## 5. Exclusion rules

Discard a candidate when accessibility is inferred only from realized events; absence of an event is treated as evidence of inaccessibility; contextual conditions needed to determine admissibility are unavailable; the unit or system boundary cannot be independently delimited; decisive identifiability depends on privileged partner access; downstream outcome, efficiency or value is needed to establish accessibility; or the search requires relaxing a frozen criterion.

## 6. Evidence classes

Class A — independent normative/operational specification.  
Class B — state/context evidence.  
Class C — realized event evidence.  
Class D — outcome/utility evidence.

For D-OPS-24 accessibility closure, Class A is necessary and Classes B/C may support reconstruction. Class D is excluded from accessibility adjudication.

## 7. Adjudication

**PASS:** all mandatory accessibility-identifiability conditions are independently closed.  
**FAIL:** at least one mandatory condition is demonstrably unavailable or invalid.  
**INCONCLUSIVE:** available evidence is insufficient to decide without additional privileged or non-specified information.

PASS means documentary accessibility identifiability only. It is not an industrial utility PASS, scientific validation, causal result, value result, or superiority result.

## 8. Search boundary

The search must be finite and auditable. Record source, domain, unit, specification type, accessibility rule, missing information, adjudication and reason for disposition. No candidate is promoted merely because it appears promising.

The search stops when the authorized scope is exhausted or when a candidate reaches the threshold required for a separately authorized IT-G1 review.

## 9. Anti-rescue controls

No search result may alter TGCV Core definitions, falsification thresholds, C01–C16 status, O3 status, causal/value claims, industrial-track evidence status, or the interpretation of prior failed/indeterminate results.

## 10. Gate relation

D-OPS-24 is a preparation/protocol surface only until separately authorized. Execution requires a recorded authorization and, where the operation changes canonical state, atomic propagation of all affected governance assets.

A D-OPS-24 result may inform a future candidate proposal or IT-G1 review, but cannot itself authorize IT-G2, industrial execution, Stage C/D, causal inference, value assessment or Core modification.

## 11. Required records before execution

Before any execution is authorized, the governance chain must contain this protocol; explicit scope and search boundary; Evidence→Claim impact assessment; execution authorization record; auditable result template; current-state validator PASS; and atomic propagation plan for any canonical transition.
