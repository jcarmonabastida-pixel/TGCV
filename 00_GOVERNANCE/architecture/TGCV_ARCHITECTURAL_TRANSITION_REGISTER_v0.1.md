# TGCV — Architectural Transition / Evidence Assessment Register v0.1

**Status:** CURRENT GOVERNANCE LAYER / NON-SCIENTIFIC-CLAIMING  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** Explicitly govern the possible transition from the inherited TGCV architecture to a Transformational Space Dynamics & Intelligence (TSDI) architecture without prematurely changing the canonical Core or Evidence→Claim Matrix.

## 1. Architectural state

The current operative architecture remains the inherited architecture recorded in RMA v3.37:

- Core ontology: `S`
- Accessible transformation space: `T_acc = F(S,C,L)`
- Central phenomenon: `ΔT_acc`
- Downstream analytical chain: `ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value`
- `I` remains an explanatory mechanism rather than a Core primitive.

**Disposition:** CURRENT WORKING BASELINE. It is not declared obsolete.

The emerging **Transformational Space Dynamics & Intelligence (TSDI)** direction is recorded here as an **architectural hypothesis under empirical evaluation**, not as a replacement Core.

## 2. Why this layer exists

Accumulated evidence, including bounded Dynamic Transformation Space evidence, can motivate a question about whether the inherited architecture is the most adequate explanatory level. The existence of such evidence does not itself authorize an architectural replacement.

This register therefore separates:

1. empirical evidence;
2. architectural interpretation of that evidence;
3. revision of the Core;
4. revision of the Evidence→Claim Matrix.

No one of these steps is inferred automatically from another.

## 3. Competing architectural descriptions

### A — inherited architecture

The system is represented through state/conditions and an accessible transformation space. Temporal change is represented primarily through changes in `T_acc`, with Reach, trajectories and downstream variables treated as derived or subsequent analytical layers.

### B — TSDI architectural hypothesis

The primary object under investigation is the **structural dynamics of the transformation space itself**, including:

- evolution of the transformation-space structure;
- persistence, appearance, disappearance and reconfiguration of transformation possibilities;
- dependencies and relations among transformations;
- trajectories through transformation space;
- conditions under which a system can identify, discriminate among, and exploit changing transformation possibilities.

This formulation remains provisional until discriminating empirical evidence supports it.

## 4. Architectural assessment rule

Future evidence must be assessed against both descriptions where the experimental design permits it.

An evidence item may be classified as:

- **FOR-A:** supports the inherited architectural explanation;
- **FOR-B:** supports the TSDI architectural explanation;
- **AGAINST-A:** exposes an explanatory or representational insufficiency of A;
- **AGAINST-B:** exposes an insufficiency of B;
- **NON-DISCRIMINATING:** compatible with both;
- **IRRELEVANT:** does not bear on the architectural distinction.

A result must not be classified as FOR-B merely because it uses TSDI terminology.

## 5. Transition criteria

A review of the canonical Core becomes warranted only when accumulated evidence provides a traceable case that one or more of the following conditions hold:

1. the inherited Core cannot represent a material observed phenomenon without ad-hoc additions that change its intended ontology;
2. the inherited Core systematically loses explanatory information that is represented naturally by transformation-space dynamics;
3. a TSDI representation yields a reproducible empirical distinction that the inherited representation cannot express or explain without equivalent reconstruction;
4. the distinction survives independent or materially different operationalizations;
5. the evidence is strong enough to justify a formal architectural review rather than merely a new terminology layer.

A Core revision is **not** triggered by a single suggestive result, a more attractive conceptual formulation, or the mere accumulation of evidence about `T_acc`.

## 6. Trigger for Evidence→Claim Matrix review

The current Matrix v1.44 remains operative until architectural review establishes that its claim ontology or claim routing is materially incompatible with the supported architecture.

A Matrix review becomes warranted when:

- a Core revision is formally proposed; or
- accumulated evidence shows that existing claim units systematically conflate distinct architectural objects; or
- a new architectural object requires claims/evidence classes that cannot be represented without distorting the existing matrix.

If a review is triggered, it must preserve cumulative traceability:

`old claim → accumulated evidence → detected architectural problem → new concept → revised claim`.

No current claim is upgraded merely because a Matrix review is opened.

## 7. Mandatory separation from scientific execution

This register:

- does not authorize any experiment;
- does not authorize data collection or analysis;
- does not upgrade any C01–C16 claim;
- does not modify RMA v3.37;
- does not modify Evidence→Claim Matrix v1.44;
- does not establish Transformational Intelligence;
- does not establish TSDI as the canonical architecture;
- does not establish a `ΔT_acc → Value` pathway.

Any future experiment must have its own falsifiable question, operationalization, controls, preflight, authorization and closure.

## 8. Current architectural assessment

**Status:** OPEN — ARCHITECTURAL TRANSITION UNDER EMPIRICAL EVALUATION.

Current evidence is sufficient to justify explicit governance of the transition question, but not sufficient in this register to declare the TSDI architecture empirically established or to replace the current Core.

The next scientific design question should therefore be discriminating rather than confirmatory:

> What observations would empirically distinguish an explanation based on the inherited accessible-transformation-space representation from one based on the structural dynamics of that space?

This question is recorded as a design criterion, not as authorization to execute an experiment.

## 9. Governance precedence

Until a formal architectural review closes this transition:

**CANONICAL_STATE → Architectural Transition Register → RMA → Evidence→Claim Matrix → RMA traceability → STATUS → validator**

The transition register is an explicit governance layer and does not supersede the scientific state held by the current RMA or Matrix.
