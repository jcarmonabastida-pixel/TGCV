# TGCV — Industrial Discovery Filter Post-IT-METH-I v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Status:** `FROZEN — DISCOVERY FILTER / NOT EXECUTION AUTHORIZATION`  
**Scope:** future industrial candidate discovery after closure of IT-METH-I

## 1. Purpose

Define the ex-ante screening filter for selecting industrial candidates that are suitable for a future comparative utility experiment.

The filter incorporates the methodological requirements derived from IT-METH-I closure. It is a discovery/admission filter only; it does not select a case, execute an experiment, or modify TGCV Core.

## 2. Mandatory gates

A candidate may advance to a formal IT-G1 review only if all mandatory gates below are PASS.

| Gate | Requirement | PASS condition | Block condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Documentary closure | Decision-relevant accessibility conditions can be frozen and evidenced independently | Critical condition depends on analyst inference or privileged information |
| F2 | System/state identifiability | System boundary, state/context and temporal frame can be defined ex ante | Boundary or state depends on expected TGCV result |
| F3 | Transformation identity | Candidate transformation has an explicit identity and admissibility rule | Transformation only inferable retrospectively |
| F4 | Accessibility observability | Material/data/configuration/dependency conditions are independently observable | Accessibility inferred from realized choice/event alone |
| F5 | Temporal closure | Relevant decision-time and temporal conditions are explicitly anchored | Critical temporal condition remains unresolved |
| F6 | Evidence integrity | Evidence can be frozen, versioned and byte/semantic anchored | Critical evidence is mutable, ambiguous or unverifiable |
| F7 | Metric observability | Every mandatory utility metric has an executable observation rule before authorization | Any mandatory metric would be indeterminate by design |
| F8 | Effort readiness | If effort is scored, start/stop convention and exclusions are frozen before blind execution | Effort convention would be supplied after observing results |
| F9 | Reproducibility readiness | Independent reconstruction unit, matching rule, indeterminate treatment and threshold are frozen | Agreement cannot be deterministically adjudicated |
| F10 | Discriminative utility potential | At least one pre-specified practical dimension offers a credible opportunity for TGCV/comparator differentiation | Both methods are expected only to recover equivalent information |
| F11 | Comparator validity | Conventional comparator is explicit, fair, reproducible and defined on the same evidence boundary | Comparator is weaker, incomplete or retrospectively optimized |
| F12 | Downstream separation | Outcome/value variables can remain outside accessibility adjudication and be observed independently where required | Post-decision outcomes are needed to define accessibility |

## 3. Hard exclusions

Discard or hold a candidate when:

- accessibility can only be reconstructed from realized choices;
- required decision-time conditions are unavailable and cannot be independently bounded;
- a utility metric cannot be observed under the planned evidence boundary;
- the case has no plausible discriminative utility dimension;
- the comparator cannot be frozen on the same evidence boundary;
- the candidate requires post-decision outcomes to define the transformation or accessibility set;
- the candidate would require changing TGCV definitions, Core primitives or thresholds merely to fit the case.

## 4. Candidate scoring discipline

Discovery screening is categorical, not a utility score.

Use only:

`PASS` — requirement satisfied by available documentary evidence;

`CONDITIONAL` — potentially satisfiable but not yet closed; candidate does not advance;

`FAIL` — requirement demonstrably unavailable or invalid;

`DISCARD` — structural incompatibility with the industrial-track objective.

No candidate may receive an overall PASS by averaging failures or by compensating one blocked gate with strength in another.

## 5. Anti-rescue rule

The discovery analyst must not:

- infer missing accessibility from downstream outcomes;
- infer accessibility from frequency of realized transformations;
- introduce unobserved contextual variables solely to rescue a candidate;
- relax a gate after seeing a promising TGCV representation;
- redefine the comparator after observing TGCV output;
- convert `CONDITIONAL` into `PASS` without new admissible evidence.

## 6. Preferred candidate profile

Discovery should preferentially target domains with:

1. explicit normative, contractual, operational or configuration specifications;
2. multiple admissible transformations or compliance/operational alternatives;
3. decision-time conditions that are documentable;
4. stable system boundaries;
5. independently observable constraints;
6. measurable practical decision/analytical work;
7. a conventional method that is already legitimate and reproducible;
8. a plausible mechanism by which representing accessibility and transformations could improve a practical task without requiring post-hoc assumptions.

## 7. Discovery output

For each screened candidate, record:

- candidate identifier;
- domain and source;
- natural unit of analysis;
- F1–F12 disposition;
- evidence references;
- missing/privileged information;
- reason for CONDITIONAL/FAIL/DISCARD;
- whether a separate IT-G1 review is justified.

Discovery does not perform IT-G1. If a candidate appears to satisfy all filters, stop screening that candidate and route it to separately governed IT-G1 review.

## 8. Relationship to current industrial-track state

The existing candidate matrix remains historical/operative for its completed discovery cycle. This filter does not retroactively alter ICD-01 through ICD-08 dispositions.

The FAA AMOC experiment remains closed `INCONCLUSIVE`; its sealed artifacts and comparison record remain immutable evidence.

## 9. Scientific boundary

This filter does not introduce scientific evidence and does not change TGCV Core.

It is specifically designed to prevent methodological limitations observed in IT-METH-I from recurring in a future industrial utility experiment.
