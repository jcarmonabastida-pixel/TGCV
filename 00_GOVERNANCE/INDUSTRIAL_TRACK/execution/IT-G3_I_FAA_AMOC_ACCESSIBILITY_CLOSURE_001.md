## 1. Purpose

Close the accessibility/admissibility criterion for the frozen AMOC case before any comparative outcome or utility assessment. The criterion is applied independently of any expected TGCV result.

## 2. Accessibility object

The bounded transformation under assessment is the use of the FAA-approved AMOC as an alternative method of compliance to AD 91-12-10, replacing the interim 15,000-hour TIS life-limit condition with the specified Airworthiness Limitations provisions, for aircraft within the explicitly approved scope.

The EASA public record exposes the AD identity, approval holder, affected aircraft types, the AMOC reference/date and the required maintenance-manual revisions. citeturn1view0

The attached FAA approval letter provides the operative accessibility conditions: identified aircraft models and serial-number ranges, applicable Airworthiness Limitations revisions, continuing applicability of all other AD provisions, U.S.-registry transferability, notification before use, and recording of AMOC use in aircraft records. citeturn2view0

## 3. Ex-ante accessibility criterion

For this case, the AMOC is classified **ACCESSIBLE / ADMISSIBLE** only when all mandatory conditions below can be established from the frozen decision record and applicable evidence:

1. The aircraft is within an explicitly listed approved model/type designation.
2. The aircraft serial number is within an explicitly listed approved range.
3. The specified Airworthiness Limitations maintenance-manual revision, or a later FAA-approved revision expressly permitted by the AMOC, is available for use.
4. All AD 91-12-10 provisions not superseded by the AMOC remain applicable and can be complied with.
5. The aircraft is operated under U.S. registry for purposes of the transferability provision, unless a separate competent-authority approval applies.
6. Before use, the required principal-inspector/FSDO notification condition is satisfied.
7. Use of the AMOC is recorded in the aircraft records as required.

Any failure to establish a mandatory condition is **INDETERMINATE / NOT ADMISSIBLE**, not silently treated as accessible.

## 5. Important boundary

This closure does **not** require the project to infer accessibility from whether an aircraft subsequently operated successfully, whether a maintenance action was later performed, or whether any economic/safety benefit occurred.

Likewise, the project does not treat later FAA-approved manual revisions as evidence that the original 2018 decision was favorable. They are admissible only because the AMOC itself explicitly permits later FAA-approved revisions.

The closure therefore concerns the **decision-defined accessibility rule**, not observed post-decision success.
