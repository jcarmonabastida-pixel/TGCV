# INDUSTRIAL-TRACK — Governance Specification v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Origin:** EXT-UPD-4.9  
**Decision class:** Design / preparation only  
**Execution status:** NOT AUTHORIZED

## 1. Purpose

The INDUSTRIAL-TRACK is a separately governed programme/application line for investigating whether TGCV can provide useful differentiated analytical capability in a real industrial decision context.

It exists to preserve the industrial question without using industrial applicability to validate, rescue, redefine, or strengthen the scientific Core.

The track is **PROPOSED**. Its creation does not constitute evidence of industrial utility, superiority, causality, prediction, financial value, or Core validation.

## 2. Research/application question

The working question is:

> Can TGCV provide a reproducible and independently assessable analytical capability that identifies decision-relevant changes in accessible transformations, and does that capability provide utility beyond an explicitly defined comparator in a bounded industrial decision context?

This question is intentionally conditional. It does not assume that TGCV is useful, superior, causal, predictive, or value-generating.

## 3. Separation from the scientific Core

The INDUSTRIAL-TRACK shall preserve the current scientific architecture:

- Core ontology: `S`.
- Analytical object: `T_acc = F(S,C,L)`.
- Central phenomenon: `ΔT_acc`.
- Downstream chain: `ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value`.
- `I` remains an explanatory mechanism, not a Core primitive.

Industrial evidence shall not be used retrospectively to modify these elements, their falsification criteria, thresholds, or epistemic status.

## 4. Minimum case-selection criteria

A candidate industrial case is admissible for design review only if all of the following can be established before execution authorization:

1. A concrete industrial decision context is identifiable.
2. The relevant system boundary can be stated explicitly.
3. The unit of analysis is bounded and reproducible.
4. The decision horizon or temporal frame is specified.
5. Relevant state/context variables are observable or defensibly reconstructable.
6. Candidate transformations and their enabling/limiting conditions are observable or defensibly reconstructable.
7. Relevant outcomes can be observed independently of the TGCV analysis.
8. Access to the necessary material is feasible under explicitly stated conditions.

Failure to satisfy any mandatory criterion blocks execution authorization; it is not grounds for relaxing the criterion after observing results.

## 5. Unit of analysis

The unit of analysis shall be defined independently of TGCV's expected result and shall permit reconstruction of at least:

- system state/context at the relevant time;
- candidate transformation(s);
- admissibility/accessibility conditions;
- constraints and dependencies;
- resulting state or decision outcome;
- temporal ordering sufficient for the intended comparison.

The unit shall be frozen before comparative evaluation.

## 6. Observable variables

The design shall distinguish at minimum:

### 6.1 System/context variables
Variables describing the relevant state and contextual conditions without encoding the expected TGCV conclusion.

### 6.2 Transformation variables
Variables identifying candidate transformations and their admissibility/accessibility conditions.

### 6.3 Outcome variables
Variables observed independently of the TGCV analysis and used only after the transformation/accessibility representation has been frozen.

### 6.4 Utility variables
Variables defining whether the analytical capability produces a practically relevant benefit. Utility variables must not be defined as synonymous with `ΔT_acc`.

## 7. Independent accessibility closure

Accessibility must be closed before comparative outcome analysis.

The closure specification shall state, ex ante:

- what counts as an admissible transformation;
- what material or conditions must be available;
- what setup/configuration conditions are required;
- what temporal constraints apply;
- what evidence is sufficient to establish accessibility;
- what evidence is insufficient or ambiguous;
- how indeterminate cases are handled.

The criterion shall be independent of whether a candidate result is favorable to TGCV.

An unresolved material/setup dependency remains **INDETERMINATE** and cannot be silently converted into PASS or FAIL.

## 8. Independent utility criterion

Utility shall be defined before outcome assessment and independently of the TGCV result.

A future protocol must specify:

1. the practical decision or analytical task;
2. the baseline/comparator capability;
3. the measurable utility dimensions;
4. the minimum practically meaningful difference, where applicable;
5. the observation period;
6. the treatment of null, adverse, and indeterminate results.

Examples of utility dimensions may include decision quality, coverage of relevant options, time-to-analysis, reproducibility, error reduction, or other case-specific measures. No dimension is adopted by this specification as evidence that TGCV is useful.

## 9. Comparative design

If differentiated utility or superiority is tested, the comparator and evaluation protocol must be frozen before outcome assessment.

The protocol shall identify:

- comparator method/capability;
- identical or explicitly justified input conditions;
- evaluation metrics;
- sampling or case-selection rules;
- stopping rules, if applicable;
- handling of ties, null results, failures, and missing data.

No comparator may be selected or redefined after observing TGCV-favorable results.

## 10. Epistemic outcomes

A future industrial execution shall use three primary outcome classes:

- **PASS:** all mandatory validity conditions are satisfied and the pre-specified utility criterion is met.
- **FAIL:** the specified validity conditions or utility criterion are not met in a way that permits a determinate negative conclusion.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** material evidence or validity conditions remain unresolved such that neither PASS nor FAIL is justified.

Procedural execution success shall not be conflated with scientific or utility PASS.

## 11. Evidence separation

Evidence shall be partitioned at minimum into:

1. **Core evidence** — evidence relevant to TGCV's scientific/ontological claims.
2. **Industrial-track evidence** — evidence relevant to the bounded industrial application question.
3. **Causal evidence** — evidence supporting causal interpretation, if any.
4. **Value evidence** — evidence supporting downstream value claims, if any.

Evidence in one class shall not automatically upgrade claims in another class.

## 12. Non-retroactivity rule

After evidence is observed, the project shall not modify:

- Core definitions;
- falsification criteria;
- accessibility thresholds;
- utility thresholds;
- comparator definition;
- inclusion/exclusion rules;
- PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE rules;

for the purpose of converting an unfavorable or indeterminate result into a favorable result.

Any genuine methodological revision requires a new explicit governance decision and must remain distinguishable from the preceding result.

## 13. Authorization gates

A future industrial execution requires sequential closure of the following gates:

### IT-G0 — Strategic admission
Confirm that the proposed case is an industrial application question and does not alter the scientific Core.

### IT-G1 — Case identifiability
Concrete case, system boundary, unit of analysis and temporal frame closed.

### IT-G2 — Variable observability
Required state, transformation, condition and outcome variables closed.

### IT-G3 — Accessibility closure
Independent accessibility/admissibility criterion closed before comparative assessment.

### IT-G4 — Utility protocol freeze
Independent utility criterion and comparator frozen before outcome assessment.

### IT-G5 — Execution authorization
Governance review confirms that IT-G0 through IT-G4 are closed and explicitly authorizes execution.

No execution is authorized by this specification itself.

## 14. Explicit exclusions under v0.1

This specification does **not** authorize:

- a new industrial experiment;
- a new dataset execution;
- further O3 accessibility execution under EXT-UPD-4.8;
- Stage-C or Stage-D execution;
- evidential engagement with an industrial partner;
- causal inference;
- value optimization;
- Core modification;
- claim-status upgrades;
- publication/application claims representing industrial utility as established;
- updates to external research/application assets as evidence of utility.
