# TGCV — Internal Programme Operating System (Recovered v0.1)

**Status:** RECOVERED / HISTORICAL-CONVERGENT
**Date:** 2026-08-27

## Purpose

This document records the internal operating architecture that can be established from recovered TGCV history and the RMA. It is **not** declared a new freeze. It is a controlled synthesis of recovered material intended to prevent loss of programme logic.

## 1. Three-level programme architecture

Recovered programme design distinguishes three levels:

### Level 1 — Architecture / governance

The programme-level architecture has been described as including:

- Proyecto Cero;
- Contrato de Trabajo;
- ACTI/ACTII-related architecture assets;
- PMA/MDP-related planning/control assets;
- SIP;
- governance and architecture review.

The exact historical naming and definitions of ACTII, PMA and MDP still require source-level recovery and therefore are not silently normalized here.

### Level 2 — Portfolio

The portfolio contains evolving research and programme assets, with explicit dependencies and states. The RMA is the current formal registry mechanism for this layer. fileciteturn60file0

### Level 3 — Execution

Recovered programme logic describes an execution cycle around the SIP: prepare/review the portfolio, select the next work, execute it, update programme state, and conduct architecture/governance review.

## 2. MOP / operational flow

Historical programme work also records an operational flow with multiple stations, beginning with exploration and progressing through structured discovery, prioritisation, connection/portfolio decisions, construction and evolution. The exact canonical station names should be recovered from the source artefact before being frozen.

## 3. SIP

The SIP is consistently represented as the programme's research operating/prioritisation system. Its confirmed role is to turn the available portfolio and research state into controlled next actions rather than allowing conversational drift to determine the programme sequence. The RMA classifies it as a methodological asset. fileciteturn60file1

## 4. PMO / SMO

PMO/SMO is the programme-control layer responsible for coordination of assets, gates, decisions, versions and dependencies. The RMA explicitly records Project Zero and the Work Contract as dependencies, so those two artefacts are treated as upstream governance sources once recovered. fileciteturn60file0

## 5. Architecture review

A recurring programme-control requirement is that work must pass through architecture/governance review and preserve the current Core. This is consistent with the recovered instruction that sprints maintain SIP, PMO and architecture/governance-review filters.

## 6. Scientific control boundary

The internal operating system cannot alter the scientific Core merely because a programme-management workflow suggests a new concept. Scientific changes require explicit research evidence/gates. The current canonical scientific architecture remains `Core=S`, with `T_acc=F(S,C,L)` and `ΔT_acc -> ΔReach -> ΔTrajectory`; the historical development record shows how earlier constructs were progressively reduced and tested. fileciteturn60file7

## 7. SLR interface

The programme OS interfaces with SLR as an evidence-producing subsystem rather than treating literature review as a one-off document. The RMA specifies expedientes, Evidence Bank, Fact Bank and SLR-1 Prior-Art Absorption Matrix as linked assets. fileciteturn60file4

## 8. Non-canonical historical material

Earlier value-construction and organisational-capability material is preserved as historical research/application provenance. It must not be promoted into the scientific Core without explicit traceability. The early research record shows the former framing in terms of actors, capabilities, relations, transformations and value, followed by a later reduction toward a stricter minimal Core. fileciteturn60file12 fileciteturn60file13
