# TGCV — Governance of the Transformation-Dynamics and Ontology Research Line

**Document ID:** TGCV_RESEARCH_LINE_TRANSFORMATIONAL_DYNAMICS_ONTOLOGY_GOVERNANCE_001  
**Status:** CANONICAL GOVERNANCE RECORD  
**Date:** 2026-09-23

## 1. Purpose

This record governs the scientific line established through the recent TR-131 discussions.

The line is considered strategically significant and must be preserved across future research operations, experimental redesigns, conceptual revisions, and doctoral/industrial planning.

It is not itself scientific evidence. It is a governance record for the research programme.

## 2. Research-line architecture

The current working architecture is:

`S_t → T_acc,t → T_real,t → S_(t+1) → T_acc,t+1 → trajectory → outcome → VSL → value`

with a capability layer concerning transformation handling / Transformational Intelligence:

`T_acc → transformation handling / TI → T_real → trajectory`

The architecture is provisional at the ontological level and operationally usable at the analytical level.

## 3. Strategic shift

The programme has moved away from:

- proving representational superiority of `T_acc` over a domain-native baseline;
- treating `ΔT_acc → ΔValue` as the central causal hypothesis;
- requiring a single domain experiment to establish transversal validity.

The programme now focuses on:

- transformation-space dynamics;
- cross-domain analytical applicability;
- practical usefulness of transformation-space information;
- transformation handling and navigation;
- Transformational Intelligence;
- downstream outcome linkage;
- independent VSL/value-guided navigation;
- eventual scientific review of the Core and ontology.

## 4. Methodological rule

The following rule is binding for this research line:

> **Evidence first → conceptual differentiation second → ontological review third → Core modification only if warranted by the accumulated evidence.**

No construct shall be promoted into the scientific Core merely because it is useful analytically, intuitive conceptually, or convenient for a new experiment.

Conversely, the existing Core must not constrain the emerging science merely because it was defined earlier.

The research programme must allow the evidence to determine whether the ontology needs to evolve.

## 5. Ontology review principle

The following constructs are candidates for future ontological review:

- `S`;
- `T_acc`;
- `T_real`;
- `selection`;
- `transformation_handling`;
- `trajectory`;
- transformation-space dynamics;
- `Transformational Intelligence`;
- context/constraints where independently necessary;
- the relation between state and future transformation space.

The review must determine for each candidate whether it is:

1. ontological primitive;
2. derived construct;
3. analytical representation;
4. mechanism;
5. capability;
6. process/event;
7. downstream evaluative construct.

No classification is predetermined.

## 6. Specific questions for future Core review

The eventual ontology review must explicitly ask:

### 6.1 State sufficiency

Is a Core centred on `S` sufficient to account for the scientific phenomena now under investigation?

### 6.2 Realization

Is `T_real` irreducible, or can it be fully reconstructed from state, transition and contextual information?

### 6.3 Selection

Does selection constitute an irreducible process/mechanism, or is it derivable from existing constructs?

### 6.4 Transformation handling

Is transformation handling merely a descriptive category, a mechanism, a capability, or a necessary scientific construct?

### 6.5 Trajectory

Is trajectory purely derived, or does the programme require trajectory as a higher-order construct?

### 6.6 Transformation-space dynamics

Can changes in `T_acc` be treated entirely as derived relations, or does the scientific explanation require a distinct dynamic construct?

### 6.7 Transformational Intelligence

Is TI best treated as a capability/emergent property, a mechanism, or a higher-order construct built from observable transformation-handling patterns?

### 6.8 Boundary with value

Can TI remain scientifically independent while supporting value-guided navigation through an external VSL?

## 7. Experimental sequence

The canonical research sequence is:

**Gate A — Cross-domain operationalisation**  
→ **Gate B — Cross-domain usefulness**  
→ **Gate C — Transformational Intelligence differentiation**  
→ **Gate D — Outcome linkage**  
→ **Gate E — Value-guided navigation**  
→ **Gate F — Scientific Core & Ontology Review**

### Gate F

Gate F is not a presupposition that the Core must change.

Its purpose is to determine, from accumulated evidence, whether:

- the Core remains adequate;
- the Core requires extension;
- some existing primitive should be demoted;
- a new primitive/process/capability is warranted;
- the analytical architecture should remain outside the ontology;
- the boundaries between mechanism, capability, process and ontology need revision.

Gate F may therefore conclude **NO ONTOLOGICAL CHANGE REQUIRED**.

## 8. Cross-domain operationalisation governance

Gate A must not be designed merely to confirm the current architecture.

It must be capable of revealing where the architecture breaks, becomes ambiguous, or requires domain-specific qualifications.

The second domain must be materially different from VisitAll and should permit observation of:

`T_acc,t → T_real,t → T_acc,t+1`

The experiment should preserve source traceability, independent reconstruction, frozen conditions and explicit claim boundaries.

The result of Gate A must therefore be capable of producing either:

- operationalisation PASS;
- operationalisation PASS WITH BOUNDARY;
- operationalisation FAIL;
- or an explicit indication that an architectural construct requires revision.

## 9. No premature ontology migration

Until Gate F, `T_real`, selection, transformation handling, trajectory and TI are to be treated as **candidate constructs at the appropriate analytical/mechanistic/capability level**, not as newly accepted Core primitives.

This does not prevent experiments from operationalising them.

## 10. Governance of future conceptual changes

Any material conceptual change must:

1. identify the evidence motivating the change;
2. state which previous assumption is being challenged;
3. distinguish empirical result from interpretation;
4. assess impact on Core, RMA, Matrix, SIP and experimental specifications;
5. create a controlled version of affected canonical assets;
6. preserve the previous version and decision trace;
7. update no-claims and claim boundaries;
8. avoid silently changing definitions used by closed experiments.

## 11. Relation to Value/VSL

Value remains downstream:

`trajectory → outcome → VSL → V*`

The research line does not reinstate `ΔT_acc → ΔValue` as a definition or assumption.

If value-guided navigation becomes empirically supported, it must remain an application/decision layer unless later evidence warrants a deeper ontological conclusion.

## 12. Governance principle

The central governance principle is:

> **Do not force the emerging science into the old ontology, and do not redesign the ontology in anticipation of evidence. Let accumulated, independently audited evidence determine whether TGCV's scientific Core must evolve.**
