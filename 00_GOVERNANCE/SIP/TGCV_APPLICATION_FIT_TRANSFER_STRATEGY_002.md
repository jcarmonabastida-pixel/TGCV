## 3. Strategic shift: from domain fit to transversal differential value

Application-fit discovery must search two complementary questions:

1. Where does TGCV map naturally onto a practical problem?
2. Where could TGCV's transversal analytical capacity be materially relevant because the problem itself is distributed across domains?

The second question is particularly important for problems in which a transformation in one domain changes the transformations, constraints, capabilities, resources or costs available in another domain, producing coupled trajectories.

The working hypothesis is:

`transformation in domain A → change in accessible transformations in domain B → coupled trajectory across domains → consequential outcome/value`

A stronger form is a **problem-of-problems** in which no single domain adequately represents the compound transformation structure.

This is an application/research hypothesis, not a demonstrated TGCV capability.

## 4. Application Fit — two dimensions

Application Fit is now explored along two dimensions rather than implicitly through sector alone.

### Dimension 1 — Domain / sector context

The operational domain(s) in which the practical problem is situated.

### Dimension 2 — Degree of transformational coupling

The extent to which the problem depends on transformations and accessibility conditions distributed across domains.

Exploratory levels:

- **D0 — Single-domain:** principal transformation space lies within one domain.
- **D1 — Adjacent-domain involvement:** multiple domains are present, but coupling may remain largely separable.
- **D2 — Cross-domain dependency:** a transformation in one domain requires conditions generated or maintained in another.
- **D3 — Coupled transformation spaces:** a state/transformation change in one domain modifies `T_acc` or admissibility conditions in another.
- **D4 — Emergent multidomain transformation:** the compound transformation cannot be adequately represented by any single domain.

These are discovery classifications, not scores, rankings, or scientific validity levels.

## 5. Hibridación transformacional — discovery patterns

WP2 should explicitly search for these eight recurring multidomain patterns:

1. **Capability propagation** — a capability created/changed in A enables transformations in B.
2. **Capability blocking** — a restriction/capability loss in A removes transformations in B.
3. **Cross-domain dependency** — A requires conditions generated by B.
4. **Cascading reconfiguration** — a transformation triggers successive changes in accessible spaces across domains.
5. **Resource coupling** — domains depend on shared resources determining accessibility.
6. **Constraint propagation** — rules, policies, safety/security constraints or technical limits cross domain boundaries.
7. **Trajectory coupling** — the subsequent trajectory in A changes the later trajectory in B, and vice versa.
8. **Emergent system transformation** — the compound transformation is not adequately represented by one domain alone.

These patterns are search instruments, not established TGCV mechanisms or scientific claims.

## 9. MOI and SIP: opportunity discovery versus epistemic state

MOI remains deliberately permissive: it may document practical transfer opportunities for which scientific evidence does not yet exist.

This does **not** weaken the evidence-driven character of the TGCV programme.

The governing distinction is:

`MOI opportunity state ≠ scientific epistemic state`

MOI may contain opportunity hypotheses, practical problems, application-fit hypotheses, multidomain-coupling hypotheses, partner routes, implementation hypotheses and evidence gaps without treating them as scientific evidence.

Whenever the programme seeks to determine whether an opportunity, deployment, observation or intervention bears on a TGCV scientific claim, the evidence question must enter a controlled SIP cycle.

The intended circuit is:

`discovery → practical opportunity → MOI record → evidence-gap identification → SIP cycle → evidence generation/audit → epistemic-state update → RMA/Matrix/STATUS update where warranted → MOI state refreshed`

Therefore:

- MOI is the broad opportunity/transfer map;
- SIP is the controlled route for evidence generation, auditing and epistemic updating;
- RMA/Matrix/STATUS remain authoritative for scientific claim state;
- no opportunity is silently promoted to evidence;
- absence of current evidence does not prevent an opportunity from being recorded.

An MOI case can consequently have high transfer relevance and low/unknown scientific evidence maturity simultaneously.

## 10. Transfer architecture implication

The transfer architecture must not be limited implicitly to monodomain applications.

A possible architecture is:

`Domain A ↔ Connector A ↔ TGCV Foundations ↔ Connector B ↔ Domain B`

and, where necessary:

`Domain A ↔ Connector A ↔ TGCV Foundations ↔ Connector B ↔ Domain B ↔ Connector C ↔ Domain C ...`

The important hypothesis is not merely common data representation. It is whether a common analytical layer can represent relations among transformation spaces while preserving domain-native semantics.

If recurring cases require fundamentally incompatible representations, that incompatibility must be retained as an architectural boundary rather than hidden by forced unification.

## 12. Scientific protection boundaries

The following remain hard boundaries:

- application opportunity is not scientific evidence;
- partner interest is not validation;
- technical feasibility is not causal evidence;
- operational benefit is not automatically TGCV Value;
- multidomain presence is not automatically transformational coupling;
- transformational coupling is not evidence of transversal validity;
- a successful connector is not evidence of transversal validity;
- application-fit classification is not a scientific score;
- scientific claims remain governed by RMA, Matrix and their evidence gates.
