# C09 — Dataset-First Discovery Conversation Record 001

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Status:** RECORDED — STRATEGIC DISCOVERY REFINEMENT  
**Scope:** C09 real-world discovery

## 1. Conversation decision

The discussion identified a structural possibility that should not be treated as an exceptional data defect: in real-world causal chains, the organisation that changes accessibility or the transformation space may be different from the organisation that subsequently records outcomes, performance or value.

Therefore, repeated failure to find a single dataset containing both the upstream structural transition and the downstream outcome must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the C09 bridge is empirically unavailable.

## 2. New methodological hypothesis

A real-world C09 chain may naturally span multiple institutional data systems:

`Source A: intervention → structural accessibility transition`

`Source B: downstream institution/system → subsequent outcome/value trajectory`

with a reproducible linkage between them:

`Source A + linkage + Source B → Z → ΔT_acc → Y`.

This is consistent with established causal data-fusion methodology, which explicitly addresses combining heterogeneous datasets collected under different conditions and populations, provided identification and transportability conditions are satisfied. citeturn0search0turn0search1

## 3. Scientific implication

The data-fusion route is **not** a relaxation of C09.

It potentially provides a more realistic empirical representation of the real-world value-construction chain, where upstream and downstream actors are institutionally separated.

However, joining two datasets does not itself establish the causal bridge. The fused design must preserve:

- stable unit linkage;
- temporal ordering;
- independently defined `T_acc`;
- independent downstream `Y`;
- treatment/intervention identification;
- confounding and selection control;
- direct/alternative pathway audit;
- and, where populations or institutions differ, defensible causal transportability.

The causal literature explicitly warns that heterogeneous-source fusion can introduce confounding, selection and cross-population bias, and provides formal criteria for when fusion is feasible. citeturn0search0turn0search3

## 4. Search-policy decision

Protocol 004 has therefore been persisted as the controlled secondary discovery route.

Primary route remains:

`single-source dataset → D6-E → D1–D5 → D6`

Secondary route:

`Source A + Source B → D6-E-F → D1-F → D5-F → D6`

The secondary route becomes preferred after repeated materially distinct D4 failures caused specifically by institutional/data-system separation, or earlier when an exceptionally strong cross-institutional candidate is found.

## 5. Interpretation boundary

The following are explicitly **not** sufficient:

- combining two unrelated papers;
- joining aggregate effects from different studies;
- assuming similar populations are transportable;
- narrative linkage without unit-level reproducibility;
- using Source B to redefine the upstream accessibility measure.

The target remains strictly:

`Z → ΔT_acc → subsequent trajectory Y`.

## 6. Relation to TGCV

This discovery refinement is methodologically relevant to TGCV because it treats the boundary between organisations and data systems as a potentially meaningful part of the empirical transformation/value chain rather than as a reason to collapse the chain into a single dataset.

No TGCV Core, RMA, Evidence Matrix or claim status is upgraded by this record.

## 7. Next operation

Continue dataset-first discovery, but record and prioritise candidates where:

1. Source A publicly identifies a structural accessibility transition;
2. Source B publicly identifies a later outcome/value trajectory;
3. a stable linkage between them is reproducible;
4. the two sources are institutionally distinct or independently generated;
5. the combined architecture has a plausible D5 mechanism-identification strategy.
