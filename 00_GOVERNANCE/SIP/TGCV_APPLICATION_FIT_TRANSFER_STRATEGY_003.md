## 1. Purpose

This version supersedes Strategy 002 as the current strategic framing for practical application-fit and transfer exploration. It preserves the mechanism-first search, multidomain transformational-coupling hypothesis, two Application-Fit dimensions, and the MOI/SIP distinction, and adds an explicit applied-demonstration/maturity track.

The purpose remains opportunity discovery and transfer maturation without converting application demand, prototype success or partner interest into scientific validation.

## 2. Product/application hypotheses

Two implementation forms remain hypotheses:

### A. TGCV Foundations Core + domain connectors

`TGCV Foundations Core + Domain Connector(s) → domain-native TGCV analytical workflow`

The Foundations Core would preserve governed distinctions among state, candidate transformations, accessibility, `T_acc`, `ΔT_acc`, Reach, Trajectory, Outcome and Value. Connectors would translate domain-native representations while preserving native semantics.

### B. Bespoke implementation

`client/system context → setup + domain/multi-domain translation protocol → domain-specific implementation → controlled deployment`

Neither form is an established product or commercial proposition.

## 3. Strategic direction: from domain fit to transversal differential value

Application-fit discovery asks two complementary questions:

1. Where does TGCV map naturally onto a practical problem?
2. Where could TGCV's transversal analytical capacity be materially relevant because the problem itself is distributed across domains?

The second question is especially relevant where a transformation in one domain changes the transformations, constraints, capabilities, resources or costs available in another domain, producing coupled trajectories.

Working hypothesis:

`transformation in domain A → change in accessible transformations in domain B → coupled trajectory across domains → consequential outcome/value`

A stronger problem-of-problems form arises where no single domain adequately represents the compound transformation structure.

This remains an application/research hypothesis, not a demonstrated TGCV capability.

## 4. Application Fit — two dimensions

### Dimension 1 — Domain / sector context

The operational domain(s) in which the practical problem is situated.

### Dimension 2 — Degree of transformational coupling

The extent to which the problem depends on transformations and accessibility conditions distributed across domains.

Exploratory levels:

- **D0 — Single-domain**
- **D1 — Adjacent-domain involvement**
- **D2 — Cross-domain dependency**
- **D3 — Coupled transformation spaces**
- **D4 — Emergent multidomain transformation**

These are discovery classifications, not scores, rankings or scientific validity levels.

## 5. Hibridación transformacional — discovery patterns

WP2 should search explicitly for:

1. capability propagation;
2. capability blocking;
3. cross-domain dependency;
4. cascading reconfiguration;
5. resource coupling;
6. constraint propagation;
7. trajectory coupling;
8. emergent system transformation.

These are search instruments, not established TGCV mechanisms or scientific claims.

## 6. Mechanism-first WP2

Mechanism classes remain:

1. opening options;
2. closing options;
3. reordering options;
4. making alternatives mutually incompatible;
5. creating capabilities;
6. removing capabilities;
7. changing transformation cost/time;
8. changing constraints determining `Pτ`.

For every candidate, WP2 should examine:

`mechanism × domain/sector × coupling degree × hibridación pattern`

and identify whether the principal problem is contextual coexistence or actual transformational coupling across domains.

The discovery question becomes:

> Where does a real operational problem involve a change that modifies what transformations become accessible afterwards, and where does that modification propagate across domains strongly enough that a transversal analytical layer could potentially be useful?

## 7. Case capture and maturation-route lens

Each candidate should record at minimum:

- problem statement;
- mechanism class(es);
- domain/sector context;
- degree of transformational coupling;
- participating domains;
- decision unit and decision-time boundary;
- state/context;
- candidate transformations;
- accessibility/admissibility relation;
- hypothesised `ΔT_acc` and cross-domain propagation;
- subsequent trajectory;
- measurable operational consequence;
- available/obtainable data;
- integration point;
- potential TGCV role;
- implementation form;
- scientific evidence maturity/gap;
- applicability maturity/gap;
- deployment/transfer maturity/gap;
- value specification/evaluation gap;
- partner ecosystem;
- proposed next proof or artefact;
- minimum viable demonstration where appropriate;
- explicit non-claims.

The key additional question is:

> **What is the immediate maturation question, and what is the smallest controlled next step capable of answering it?**

## 8. MOI versus evidence-driven epistemic control

MOI remains permissive and may contain practical transfer opportunities for which scientific evidence does not yet exist.

The governing distinction remains:

`MOI opportunity state ≠ scientific epistemic state`

MOI may document opportunity hypotheses, application fit, multidomain coupling, implementation hypotheses, partner routes and evidence gaps.

When the programme asks whether an opportunity, observation, intervention or deployment bears on a TGCV scientific claim, the question enters a controlled SIP cycle.

The evidence-driven circuit remains:

`discovery → practical opportunity → MOI record → evidence-gap identification → SIP cycle → evidence generation/audit → epistemic-state update → RMA/Matrix/STATUS update where warranted → MOI state refreshed`

Thus MOI is the opportunity/transfer map, while SIP remains the controlled route for scientific evidence and epistemic updating.

## 9. New parallel track: Applied Demonstration / Maturity

As TGCV matures, not every practical opportunity needs a scientific-evidence study as its immediate next step. Some opportunities are better advanced first through a bounded artefact that asks whether TGCV can actually be instantiated and used for the concrete problem.

The proposed parallel route is:

`MOI opportunity → applicability question → Applied Demonstration Cycle → demo/mockup/prototype/artefact → applicability-state update`

Possible artefacts include:

- mockup or wireframe;
- simulator;
- synthetic-data demonstrator;
- domain connector prototype;
- cross-domain transformation mapper;
- small proof-of-concept;
- workflow plugin;
- digital-twin/sandbox component;
- bounded decision-support prototype.

The artefact should have a defined scope, version, reproducible configuration where relevant, explicit assumptions, and explicit non-claims.

A successful artefact can demonstrate bounded practical instantiability, representation, interoperability, analytical workflow feasibility or operational usefulness. It does not automatically establish:

- a TGCV scientific claim;
- causal validity;
- transversal scientific validity;
- generalisability;
- TGCV Value.

## 10. Four distinct maturation questions

Application/transfer maturity is now understood through four distinct questions:

### Scientific

`Is the claim supported by identifiable and reproducible evidence?`

Route:
`Scientific question → SIP → scientific evidence → scientific epistemic state`

### Applicability

`Can TGCV be instantiated and used meaningfully for the concrete problem?`

Route:
`Applicability question → Applied Demonstration Cycle → artefact → applicability state`

### Deployment / transfer

`Can the demonstrated approach operate within a real organisational or technical environment?`

Route:
`Deployment question → Pilot / Integration Cycle → transfer state`

### Value

`Does the application generate measurable value under an independently specified valuation framework?`

Route:
`Value question → Independent Value Evaluation → value state`

These tracks may interact, but none substitutes for another.

## 11. Applied Demonstration control principles

The Applied Demonstration / Maturity Track should be governed separately from the scientific evidence track.

At minimum, a controlled demonstration should identify:

1. the concrete applicability question;
2. the bounded problem context;
3. the TGCV representation being instantiated;
4. inputs/state/context assumptions;
5. candidate transformations and accessibility rule where relevant;
6. expected observable demonstration behaviour;
7. artefact version and reproducibility information;
8. integration boundary;
9. operational observation/metric, if used;
10. limitations and explicit non-claims;
11. whether the result is exploratory, functional, integrated or operationally tested;
12. the next maturation decision.

The programme should avoid treating demonstration maturity as a scientific score or using it to rank scientific claims.

## 12. Multidomain demonstrations

The applied-demonstration route is potentially especially informative for multidomain opportunities.

A candidate **TGCV Cross-Domain Transformation Mapper** could connect network, cloud and AI-agent states and show, in a bounded synthetic or controlled environment, how a transformation in one domain changes transformations accessible in another and how the resulting changes propagate through a coupled workflow.

This would test practical instantiability of the transversal architecture. It would not by itself demonstrate universal transversal validity or differential value.

## 13. Transfer architecture

The transfer architecture must not be limited implicitly to monodomain applications.

Possible structure:

`Domain A ↔ Connector A ↔ TGCV Foundations ↔ Connector B ↔ Domain B`

and, where needed:

`Domain A ↔ Connector A ↔ TGCV Foundations ↔ Connector B ↔ Domain B ↔ Connector C ↔ Domain C ...`

The relevant hypothesis is whether a common analytical layer can represent relations among transformation spaces while preserving domain-native semantics.

## 14. Implications for MOI and the seven key assets

MOI should remain an opportunity space, but candidate entries should increasingly include the appropriate maturation route and, where useful, a minimum viable demonstration.

The future controlled revisions of Vision Paper, Research Prospectus, ARM and TCP should make room for:

- non-monodomain transfer architecture;
- multidomain transformational coupling as an explicit application hypothesis;
- a distinction between scientific evidence and applied demonstration;
- multiple maturation routes from opportunity to applicability, deployment and value evaluation.

These revisions must not imply that differential multidomain value or applied demonstration capability has already been demonstrated.

Substantive asset changes require controlled new versions; historical versions remain immutable.

## 15. Strategic maturity model

For practical governance, an opportunity may carry several descriptive maturity states in parallel rather than one overall score:

`Scientific state`
`Applicability state`
`Transfer/deployment state`
`Value state`

An opportunity can therefore be scientifically immature but highly suitable for a bounded applicability demonstrator, or technically demonstrable while still lacking the evidence required for a scientific claim or independently specified value evaluation.

This prevents premature collapse of different epistemic and operational questions into a single maturity label.

## 16. Scientific protection boundaries

The following remain hard boundaries:

- application opportunity is not scientific evidence;
- demo/prototype success is not scientific validation;
- technical feasibility is not causal evidence;
- operational benefit is not automatically TGCV Value;
- multidomain presence is not automatically transformational coupling;
- transformational coupling is not evidence of transversal validity;
- a successful connector is not evidence of transversal validity;
- partner interest is not validation;
- application-fit classification is not a scientific score;
- deployment maturity is not scientific validity;
- value evaluation does not retroactively redefine the scientific Core;
- scientific claims remain governed by RMA, Matrix and controlled evidence gates.
