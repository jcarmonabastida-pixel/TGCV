# Research Prospectus — PhD in Complex Systems

**Programme:** PhD in Complex Systems for Societal and Technological Transitions — IE University  
**Working research title:** A Transversal Analytical Framework for Interaction-Driven Changes in Future Transformation Possibilities  
**Source:** IE_PhD_Candidatura_Final_Pack_REVISED_v4.zip (ChatGPT Library)

## Research problem

Can a transversal analytical layer be established that explains how particular interactions or mechanisms modify the conditions of a system’s future transformations, and relate those modifications to subsequent trajectories and the creation of value?

## Research motivation

The research emerged from repeated observation of complex technological and organisational systems in which interactions do more than produce an immediate change of state: they may alter what the system can subsequently do. The project therefore shifts attention from outcomes alone to the evolution of future transformation possibilities.

## Scientific hypothesis

A cautious, falsifiable hypothesis is that interactions can modify the set of transformations accessible to a system, and that this modification can be represented in a way that is sufficiently general to be examined across domains. The project does not assume that a unified phenomenon or theory has already been established.

## Conceptual direction

The earlier application version explored system, state and interaction as candidate primitives and treated transformation possibilities, capabilities, learning and value as derived properties. This document is retained as an application-history artefact; the canonical scientific Core is maintained separately in `01_CORE/`.

## Candidate representation

For a system in state `S₀` with an accessible transformation set `A₀`, an interaction `I` may produce a new state `S₁` associated with a different accessible transformation set `A₁`: `I: (S₀,A₀) → (S₁,A₁)`. Expansion, reduction, reorganisation and recombination were candidate modes under investigation at this application stage.

## Literature landscape

The project connects research traditions including self-adaptive systems, adaptive dynamic systems, changeability, affordances, dynamic capabilities, self-X systems, runtime composition, learning, evolution and intelligent or multi-agent systems. It does not assume these literatures are disconnected or already unified.

## Research programme

1. Systematic literature review and conceptual mapping.
2. Ontological analysis and definition of minimal constructs.
3. Formalisation of the analytical layer and derivation of propositions.
4. Empirical and/or computational validation across selected contexts.
5. Examination of links between changes in future transformation possibilities, trajectories and value construction.

## Potential contribution

A defensible analytical layer for studying interaction-driven changes in future transformation possibilities, with explicit limits and testable propositions. A practical contribution may later involve methods for reasoning about transformation capacity and value, conditional on research results.

## Research stance

The doctoral project is explicitly falsifiable. Its purpose is not to confirm a pre-existing theory, but to determine whether the proposed abstraction is coherent, adds explanatory value beyond existing accounts, and where it fails.
