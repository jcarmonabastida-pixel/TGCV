## 1. Decision

**Disposition: READY FOR BOUNDED DRAFTING, WITH EXPLICIT EVIDENCE AND CLAIM BOUNDARIES.**

The authoritative source base is sufficient to explain:

- the analytical meaning of an accessible transformation space;
- why accessibility is derived from system state and relevant conditions rather than being an independent ontological primitive;
- the distinction between candidate transformations, accessible transformations, realised transformations, successor states and trajectories;
- why accessibility change is not reducible to a change in cardinality;
- the descriptive forms of transformation-space reconfiguration;
- what a bounded cross-domain study demonstrates about operationalising changes in the accessible transformation space;
- what cannot currently be inferred about Value from accessibility evidence.

## 3. Concepts the chapter can teach

### 3.1 System state and relevant conditions

Introduce `S` as the relevant system state/configuration. Explain that contextual conditions `C` and constraints/resources `L` matter to the operational analysis but are not additional ontological primitives in the current Core.

Do not turn this into a full systems-theory survey; Chapter 1 provides the wider systems foundation.

### 3.2 Candidate transformations and accessibility

Explain the distinction between:

- a candidate transformation `τ` in a declared candidate universe;
- the conditions under which it is admissible;
- the accessible transformation space `T_acc`;
- the transformation actually realised `T_real`.

The current Core expresses the analytical relation as:

`T_acc = F(S,C,L)`

and:

`τ ∈ T_acc iff P_τ(S,C,L)=1`

The operational accessibility predicate must be specified independently of downstream outcomes, realised transformations and Value. Do not imply that every application must enumerate every conceivable transformation in advance; operational closure depends on the case and must be justified.

### 3.3 Accessibility is not realisation

The formal specification distinguishes:

1. **Accessibility:** what can be transformed under the declared conditions.
2. **Realisation:** what transformation actually occurs.
3. **Trajectory:** the ordered sequence of realised transformations and resulting states.
4. **Outcome:** a domain-specific downstream result.
5. **Value:** an evaluated consequence requiring an additional argument.

These levels must not be defined retrospectively from a later level. A system can have an accessible transformation that it does not realise. Equal or equivalent accessible spaces do not, by themselves, imply that two systems will realise the same transformation.

### 3.4 Accessibility change is not a single scalar

Explain that a change in transformation-space structure can be sign-neutral and may involve expansion, contraction, reconfiguration or substitution. Equal cardinality does not imply identical transformation membership.

The analytical study of transformation-space dynamics provides the following descriptive measures where the domain representation supports them:

- `A_t`: source accessibility cardinality;
- `A_t+1`: successor accessibility cardinality;
- `G_t`: added transformation identities;
- `L_t`: removed transformation identities;
- `P_t`: persistent identities;
- `R_t = G_t + L_t`: identity turnover;
- `D_t = A_t+1 - A_t`: net cardinality change.

The chapter should introduce the meaning, not overload the reader with every notation at once. A worked example and a diagram should carry the explanation.

The six descriptive configurations are:
- stability;
- identity turnover with cardinality conservation;
- net increase with turnover;
- net decrease with turnover;
- pure addition;
- pure removal.

They are descriptive configurations, not rankings or assessments of quality.

### 3.5 Dynamics over time

Use the sequence:

`S_t → T_acc,t → T_real,t → S_(t+1) → T_acc,t+1`

Explain the distinction between the accessibility space at a point in time and its subsequent reconfiguration. The trajectory is generated by realised transformations and resulting state transitions; `T_acc` is not itself the trajectory.

The proposition that realised transformations and resulting states may alter future accessibility is a research hypothesis whose status depends on the evidence and context. Do not present every arrow in the sequence as a universal causal law.

## 4. Evidence that can be used

### 4.1 A bounded cross-domain study — operationalising changes in accessible transformations

The current evidence register classifies the cross-domain study as **closed, bounded methodological evidence**.

- 36 records across two computational domains (20 in one domain and 16 in the other).
- The computational validation reported no invalid records.
- The independent mathematical audit reports 36 checked records and zero descriptor mismatches.
- Both domains exhibit transformation-identity turnover.
- One examined computational domain exhibits changes in transformation identity while preserving the total number of transformations.
- The other examined computational domain exhibits changes in transformation identity, including some net reductions in the number of transformations.

This supports the bounded statement that a common transition-level analytical representation can operationalise transformation-space reconfiguration in these two examined domains. It does not establish universal domain independence, causal value effects, superiority or Transformational Intelligence.

Use the current evidence register as the controlling account of the finding; use the analytical study of transformation-space dynamics for definitions of the descriptive measures.

### 4.2 A bounded real-world causal study — accessibility and subsequent trajectories

The current Evidence-to-Claim Matrix v1.47 records **bounded empirical causal support** for the proposition that accessibility changes can causally affect subsequent trajectories in a studied setting. The material case concerns randomized early expansion of a rural financial-services network. The intervention changed the local service infrastructure and introduced structural access to formal financial services; the accessibility representation was kept separate from subsequent take-up and household/economic variables.

The chapter may use this as the principal real-world example of a bounded accessibility-to-trajectory relationship. It must describe the result as specific to the studied setting and intervention. It does not establish universal generality, a universal transformation-space metric, or a causal/predictive relationship between accessibility change and Value.

### 4.3 A bounded real-world boundary case — partial identification of transformation space

The current evidence register also includes a reconstruction of a street-paving intervention in Mexico. Candidate transformation functions can be specified from the intervention's ex-ante functional description, and longitudinal structural/connectivity changes can be reconstructed. However, the available evidence does not independently identify the full accessibility predicate, complete `T_acc` or `ΔT_acc`.

This case is useful because it demonstrates that an observed infrastructure intervention and a change in physical connectivity do not, by themselves, establish which transformations were accessible. The chapter must not present it as positive evidence of a fully reconstructed transformation-space change or as a causal accessibility-to-trajectory result.

### 4.4 Synthetic and cross-domain methodological evidence

The bounded cross-domain computational study contains 36 transition records across two structurally different domains. Its reconstruction and independent descriptor audit report no invalid records and no descriptor mismatches. It supports the bounded operationalisation of a common transition-level representation while preserving domain-specific transformation semantics; it does not establish universal domain independence, representational superiority or a claim-level upgrade.

A separate paired synthetic study shows that a controlled increase in the specified accessible transformation space can coexist with an unchanged realised trajectory and no change in the specified Value outcome. This is a useful teaching illustration of non-equivalence, not real-world causal evidence or proof that accessibility changes generally leave trajectories or Value unchanged.

The chapter should use only evidence that clarifies a specific concept. It must not become an exhaustive catalogue of research activity; more detailed evidence belongs in the Research section and the book's research and evidence guide.

## 5. Claims and formulations to avoid

The chapter must not say or imply:

- TGCV has established a universal theory of transformation.
- `T_acc` is an independent ontological primitive or causal force.
- More accessible transformations are always better.
- A larger transformation space necessarily means more capability, intelligence, reach or value.
- Accessibility, execution, adoption, outcome and Value are interchangeable.
- The entire chain `ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value` is empirically demonstrated in every domain.
- The bounded cross-domain study proves causal effects or generality beyond the operationalisations actually examined.
- The real-world study proves a causal relationship between changes in the accessible transformation space and Value.
- The descriptive measures of transformation-space dynamics constitute a universal scalar metric or quality score.
- The candidate formalism is demonstrably novel or superior to planning, affordance or transition-system formalisms.

Preferred formulation: TGCV investigates and formalises the structure and dynamics of transformations accessible to systems under specified conditions; current evidence supports bounded operationalisations and specific links, while wider generality and downstream Value relationships remain open to further testing.

This is a drafting outline, not a replacement for the approved detailed TOC. It keeps Chapter 3 focused and avoids duplicating the full contents of Chapters 2, 4 and 5.

7. **What bounded evidence shows — and what it does not**

8. **Limits, assumptions and open questions**  
   Discuss domain-specific operationalisation, transformation identity, admissibility, equivalence and the limits of downstream inference.

### 13.3 Temporal dynamics in software packages — 516,061 adjacent pairs

A separate empirical analysis of package-version histories provides a much larger bounded observation of transformation-space dynamics:

- 516,061 adjacent temporal pairs at horizon H=1.
- 438,203 pairs (approximately 84.91%) are classified as non-persistent.
- Classification counts: 77,858 persistence; 8,295 expansion; 3,786 contraction; 426,122 reconfiguration.
- In 159,921 pairs, `ΔT_acc ≠ 0` while bounded potential one-step Reach does not change (ND-1).
- In 278,282 pairs, both `ΔT_acc` and bounded potential one-step Reach change (ND-2).
- In 266,201 pairs, Reach cardinality is equal while Reach membership differs (ND-4).

The result supports, within the frozen software-package representation and H=1, the empirical distinction between changes in the accessible transformation set and changes in bounded potential one-step Reach. It also shows why cardinality alone cannot represent identity. It does **not** establish observed runtime reachability, H>1 trajectory sufficiency, causal effects, adoption, downstream outcomes, Value, predictive superiority or validity across domains.

This is a separate analysis from the 36-record, two-domain operationalisation study. The two must not be merged into a single sample or presented as replications of the same design.

### 13.5 Negative/non-detectable bounded causal test — urban infrastructure in Mexico

A separate TGCV reanalysis of the OpenICPSR 113705 V1 replication package reconstructed a bounded structural transformation space from six infrastructure dimensions and a frozen universe of 12 elementary opening/closure transformation families. It then estimated the pre-specified ITT effect of randomized treatment assignment on the net cardinality change of that bounded space, using 342 polygons and municipality-clustered inference.

Primary estimate:

- `β_ITT = −0.0314`
- Cluster standard error: `0.1646`
- Normal-reference p-value: `0.8486`
- 95% normal-reference confidence interval: `[−0.3540, 0.2912]`
- Wild-cluster-bootstrap p-value: `0.8354`
- 95% wild-cluster-bootstrap interval: `[−0.3087, 0.2458]`

The result is a **negative/non-detectable bounded causal result** for the frozen estimand and operationalisation. It is not evidence that the true effect is exactly zero, does not refute TGCV, and does not establish that the treatment had no structural effects. The interval remains compatible with effects in both directions. Interference across municipalities remains unresolved; the mechanistic ex-ante accessibility predicate is also an evidence gap. No Value regression was performed.

Crucially, this is a TGCV secondary reconstruction and causal test using the published study's replication package. It must not be confused with the original paper's reported programme effects on infrastructure access, housing investment or property values. The chapter should make clear that the TGCV result concerns its separately frozen structural estimand, not a reinterpretation of the original authors' headline outcomes.

**Primary bibliographic record:** Craig McIntosh, Tito Alegría, Gerardo Ordóñez and René Zenteno (2018), “The Neighborhood Impacts of Local Infrastructure Investment: Evidence from Urban Mexico,” *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*, 10(3), 263–286. DOI: `10.1257/app.20160429`. Replication package: OpenICPSR 113705 V1.

**Canonical TGCV closure:** `00_GOVERNANCE/SIP/C10C002_BOUNDED_CAUSAL_EXPERIMENT_CLOSURE_001.md` (retrieved revision `aefcaee8b23034a9a4a274456d988e4d4b5aea4b`).

### 13.6 Partial-identification boundary — street paving and connectivity

The Acayucan, Mexico street-paving case is distinct from the urban-infrastructure study in Section 13.5. Its primary source is González-Navarro and Quintana-Domeque, *Paving Streets for the Poor: Experimental Analysis of Infrastructure Effects*, with replication source Harvard Dataverse DOI `10.7910/DVN/6TC8RO`.

The source permits ex-ante specification of candidate functions associated with vehicle, pedestrian and cyclist access, parking and commercial delivery, and longitudinal reconstruction of structural/connectivity change. However, the available evidence does not independently identify the full accessibility predicate, complete `T_acc`, or `ΔT_acc`. Several observed variables are downstream outcomes or resource states and cannot be used to define accessibility.

Use this as a methodological boundary case: an intervention and observed connectivity change do not by themselves identify the full accessible transformation space. Do not present it as positive identification of complete `T_acc` or as causal accessibility-to-trajectory evidence.

**Canonical source:** `00_GOVERNANCE/SIP/TGCV_MT5_DYNAMIC_TRANSFORMATION_SPACE_RECONSTRUCTION_RESULT_001.md` (retrieved revision `a6c0df22a8b6426b19e0cab67e62038681c8f1f5`).

### 13.7 Paired synthetic non-equivalence illustration

Retain the paired synthetic example as a teaching illustration: the specified accessible transformation space increases while the frozen realised trajectory and specified Value outcome remain unchanged. It demonstrates logical non-equivalence within that construction. It is not real-world evidence, does not show that accessibility changes generally leave trajectories or Value unchanged, and does not refute the bounded C09 result.

### 13.8 Recommended evidence composition for Chapter 3

The chapter should not become an experiment catalogue. Each case must have a distinct explanatory function:

| Teaching function | Evidence to use | Reader-facing interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Show a common operational representation across structurally different domains | 36-record cross-domain study | Bounded methodological operationalisation |
| Show that cardinality misses identity and that accessibility change differs from potential one-step Reach change | 516,061 adjacent software-package pairs | Bounded structural empirical analysis at H=1 |
| Show a real-world causal accessibility-to-trajectory relation | Rural financial-services branch expansion | Bounded empirical causal support in one studied setting |
| Show that a pre-specified structural causal test can return a non-detectable result | Urban infrastructure in Mexico | Negative/non-detectable result for one frozen structural estimand; not zero-effect proof or refutation |
| Show the boundary between candidate functions, connectivity change and identified accessibility | Street paving in Acayucan | Partial identification only |
| Show non-equivalence between accessibility, realisation, trajectory and Value | Paired synthetic construction | Logical/teaching illustration, not empirical generalisation |

Use only the cases necessary to make these distinctions clear. A compact evidence table or short boxed case summaries may be preferable to six full narrative case studies.

### 13.9 Bibliographic and public-exposure controls
