# TGCV — Transformational Dynamics Formalization Proposal v0.2

**Status:** DEVELOPMENT DRAFT — NON-CANONICAL  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Supersedes for development:** v0.1  
**Purpose:** resolve the six issues identified by Audit 001 while preserving the stabilized TGCV Core and keeping Transformational Dynamics separate from the legacy downstream chain.

## 1. Governance boundary

This is a development artifact. It does not revise the current Core, RMA, Evidence-to-Claim Matrix, traceability state, or any closed experiment.

The existing relation `ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value` remains part of the current canonical architecture, but it is **not** the defining architecture of Transformational Dynamics.

## 2. Typed formal object

At analytical time `t`, define:

`Ω_T,t = (U_t, ≡_t, R_t, A_t)`

where:

- `U_t`: candidate transformation universe, independently specified or generated by a frozen rule;
- `≡_t`: canonical identity/equivalence relation over `U_t`;
- `R_t`: structural relation set over canonical transformation identities;
- `A_t`: admissibility/accessibility relation, when empirically identifiable, mapping relevant system/context information to transformation identities.

These components have distinct roles:

- `U_t` answers what candidates are represented;
- `≡_t` answers when two representations denote the same transformation;
- `R_t` answers how transformations are structurally related;
- `A_t` answers which transformations are admissible/accessible under declared conditions.

The object is therefore not reducible to the cardinality of an accessible set.

Where the accessibility formulation is identifiable:

`T_acc,t = { [τ]_≡t ∈ U_t/≡_t | A_t([τ], S_t,C_t,L_t)=1 }`

This is a derived component of the dynamic representation, not its sole definition.

## 3. Snapshot comparability

Two snapshots `Ω_T,t` and `Ω_T,u` are directly comparable only if:

1. the canonical identity rule is the same;
2. the analytical level and transformation semantics are the same;
3. there is an explicit correspondence `κ_tu` between their candidate universes, or a frozen common universe `U*` exists;
4. the structural relation types being compared are the same.

If these conditions fail, the result is **NON-COMPARABLE**, not automatically expansion, contraction, substitution, or reconfiguration.

For changing candidate universes, comparison must use either:
- a frozen common universe `U*`; or
- an explicitly frozen mapping `κ_tu` with documented additions/removals and identity uncertainty.

## 4. Structural transition

The primary object of dynamics is:

`Ω_T,t --Γ_t--> Ω_T,t+1`

where `Γ_t` is a declared comparison/transition rule over the two snapshots.

Detection of a transition must not require an explanatory mechanism variable.

Observed event/intervention information `E_t` may be recorded separately:

`(Ω_T,t, Ω_T,t+1, E_t)`

Mechanism hypotheses `M_t` are explanatory variables studied **after** the structural transition has been identified; they are not required inputs to define `Ω_T` or detect its change.

## 5. Structural change descriptors

The classes are explicitly **compositional descriptors**, not mutually exclusive labels:

- **Persistence:** no detected structural difference under the frozen comparison rule.
- **Expansion:** one or more canonical transformation identities or structural relations are newly present.
- **Contraction:** one or more canonical transformation identities or structural relations are absent relative to the prior snapshot.
- **Reconfiguration:** identities remain comparable while structural relations among them change.
- **Substitution:** a documented identity correspondence indicates replacement of one transformation role by another; substitution may therefore contain both contraction and expansion.
- **Conditional reorganization:** the function mapping declared system/context conditions to transformation-space structure changes under a frozen comparison protocol.

A single transition may carry multiple descriptors.

## 6. Representation invariance

A claimed transformation-space change must survive a pre-specified encoding perturbation that preserves the declared transformation semantics.

The empirical specification must define at least one admissible alternative representation `E'` and test:

`D(Ω_T^E,t, Ω_T^E,t+1) = D(Ω_T^{E'},t, Ω_T^{E'},t+1)`

for the relevant change descriptor or for a pre-specified equivalence class of descriptors.

If the detected change disappears solely because of an arbitrary encoding choice, the result is **representation-sensitive** and cannot be treated as evidence of transformation-space dynamics without reformulation.

## 7. Structural null

Each empirical test must include, where feasible, a null condition in which the underlying transformation-space structure is held fixed while superficial state, context, measurement presentation, or unrelated system variables may change.

The test must therefore distinguish:

- genuine change in `Ω_T`;
- apparent change induced by state/context variation with fixed `Ω_T`;
- apparent change induced by encoding or measurement variation.

Failure to distinguish these constitutes failure of identification for the tested domain.

## 8. Information firewall and temporal precedence

Construction and comparison of `Ω_T` at time `t` may use only information declared available by the frozen observation boundary.

Future execution, realized trajectory, downstream outcome, and value may not be used to construct `U_t`, `≡_t`, `R_t`, or `A_t`.

Any retrospective reconstruction that requires such information is classified as **NON-IDENTIFIED** for Transformational Dynamics.

## 9. Falsification / failure conditions

The formalization must be rejected or reformulated in a target domain if:

1. `U_t` cannot be independently specified;
2. canonical identity cannot be reproduced;
3. snapshots cannot be made temporally comparable;
4. the detected change is fully reducible to a pre-existing state variable;
5. the detected change is encoding-sensitive;
6. the structural null produces the same apparent dynamics as the proposed positive case;
7. reconfiguration cannot be distinguished from cardinality-only change where that distinction is claimed;
8. construction requires future execution, trajectory, outcome, or value information;
9. domain translation requires arbitrary semantic relabelling that destroys the declared structural roles.

## 10. Domain-independent versus domain-specific components

**Domain-independent candidate structure:**
- snapshot tuple `Ω_T`;
- identity/equivalence role;
- structural-relation role;
- accessibility/admissibility role;
- comparability requirement;
- compositional change descriptors;
- representation-invariance requirement;
- structural-null requirement;
- information firewall;
- falsification logic.

**Domain-specific components:**
- candidate transformation vocabulary;
- observation unit;
- empirical encoding;
- structural relation semantics;
- measurement instruments;
- uncertainty model;
- domain-specific admissibility conditions.

Domain-specific instantiation must preserve the functional roles without requiring arbitrary reinterpretation of the domain-independent structure.

## 11. Development status

This v0.2 resolves the six findings from Audit 001 at the proposal level. It does **not** establish empirical validity and is not yet a frozen operational specification.

Before execution, a separate freeze gate must specify one concrete domain, instantiate every component, pre-register the comparison and null tests, and demonstrate that the required data exist without retrospective outcome leakage.
