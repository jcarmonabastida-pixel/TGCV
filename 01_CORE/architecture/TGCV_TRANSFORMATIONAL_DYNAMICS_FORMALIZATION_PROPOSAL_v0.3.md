# TGCV — Transformational Dynamics Formalization Proposal v0.3

**Status:** DEVELOPMENT DRAFT — NON-CANONICAL  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** resolve Audit 002 findings before any freeze decision.

## 1. Primary object

At analytical time t:

Ω_T,t = (U_t, ≡_t, R_t)

where:

- U_t is the candidate transformation universe under a frozen construction rule;
- ≡_t is the canonical identity/equivalence relation;
- R_t is the typed structural relation system over canonical transformation identities.

Accessibility/admissibility is **not part of the mandatory primary object**. Where independently identifiable, it is a derived layer A_t over Ω_T,t and declared system/context information.

Thus Transformational Dynamics can be identified without assuming that full T_acc is observable.

## 2. Structural relation signature

R_t is a finite or otherwise explicitly bounded typed relational structure:

R_t = {r_k,t}

Each relation type r_k has a frozen signature specifying:

- domain and codomain;
- directionality;
- whether multiplicity, weight, or ordering is meaningful;
- admissible value domain;
- equality rule;
- missingness/uncertainty representation.

Two relation structures are equal only under the same signature and canonical identity correspondence.

A reconfiguration exists only when canonical transformation identities remain comparable and at least one declared relation differs under that equality rule.

## 3. Snapshot comparability

Ω_T,t and Ω_T,u are comparable only if:

1. identity rules are compatible;
2. analytical level and transformation semantics are fixed;
3. a frozen common universe U* or explicit correspondence κ_tu exists;
4. relation signatures are compatible.

Otherwise the result is NON-COMPARABLE.

## 4. Structural transition

Dynamics is the comparison:

Ω_T,t --Γ_t--> Ω_T,t+1

Γ_t is a pre-specified comparison rule; it does not require an explanatory mechanism.

Observed events/interventions E_t are external annotations. Mechanism hypotheses M_t are downstream explanatory variables and cannot define Ω_T or its change.

## 5. Change descriptors

Descriptors are compositional:

- Persistence: no declared structural difference.
- Expansion: canonical identities and/or relation instances newly present.
- Contraction: canonical identities and/or relation instances absent.
- Reconfiguration: identities comparable, with changed relation structure.
- Conditional reorganization: the structural object itself differs across declared condition-indexed snapshots under a frozen condition-comparison protocol.

**Substitution is not a primitive descriptor.** If a frozen identity correspondence identifies role replacement, it is represented by contraction + expansion plus the correspondence metadata.

## 6. Conditional reorganization

Conditional reorganization is not defined as a difference in observed outcomes or profiles.

It requires:

1. at least two pre-specified condition states c1 and c2;
2. comparable Ω_T snapshots or condition-indexed structural representations;
3. a declared structural statistic/operator F(Ω_T,c);
4. a difference F(Ω_T,c1) ≠ F(Ω_T,c2) under the frozen structural equality rule;
5. a null model in which the transformation-space structure is invariant across conditions while observed state/profile variables may vary.

Ordinary conditional heterogeneity is not sufficient.

## 7. Representation invariance

Let E be the canonical encoding and φ an admissible semantic-preserving representation transformation.

φ must preserve:

- canonical transformation identity;
- relation signatures;
- relation membership/equality;
- declared structural semantics.

A result is representation-invariant only if the declared change descriptor is preserved under every admissible φ in the pre-specified perturbation class.

If different admissible encodings produce different structural conclusions, the result is REPRESENTATION-SENSITIVE.

## 8. Structural null

The null is defined at the level of Ω_T, not outcomes:

H0: Ω_T,c1 = Ω_T,c2 under the frozen identity, relation, and correspondence rules.

A domain may instantiate H0 by controlled or naturally occurring observations where the transformation-space construction rule is held fixed while state/context/profile variables are allowed to vary.

The null is not established by observing similar outcomes; it is established by the pre-specified construction of the transformation-space representation.

If the domain cannot instantiate or justify the null without assuming Ω_T invariance, identification is unresolved.

## 9. State-reducibility criterion

A claimed transformation-space difference must contain structural information not captured by the declared state representation S.

Define a frozen comparison class C_S consisting of admissible state-only representations using the variables declared available at the observation boundary.

A structural signal is state-reducible if a member of C_S reproduces the claimed change decision under the same pre-specified decision rule.

If state-only representations reproduce the decision, the result is classified STATE-REDUCIBLE rather than independent evidence of transformation-space dynamics.

No post-hoc selection of state variables is permitted.

## 10. Falsification / failure

The domain instantiation fails identification if:

1. U_t cannot be independently specified;
2. ≡_t cannot be reproduced;
3. relation signatures/equality cannot be frozen;
4. snapshots are non-comparable;
5. the result is representation-sensitive;
6. the structural null cannot be instantiated or justified without circularity;
7. the result is state-reducible;
8. conditional reorganization cannot be separated from ordinary conditional heterogeneity;
9. future execution, trajectory, outcome, or value information is required to construct Ω_T;
10. arbitrary semantic relabelling is required to obtain the claimed structure.
