# TGCV — Transformation-Space Dynamics Formal Specification 001

**Status:** CANDIDATE — FORMAL / NOT EXPERIMENTAL
**Scientific execution:** NOT AUTHORIZED
**Date:** 2026-09-20

## 1. Purpose
Define the candidate formal object of transformation-space dynamics without claiming novelty, causality, or Core status.

## 2. Starting point
TR-131 established that the tested representation `(S,C,T_acc)` can remain equal while realized transformations differ. This motivates studying `T_acc` as an explicit object, but does not establish that it is ontologically fundamental.

## 3. Formal objects
Let:
- `S_t` = system state at time `t`;
- `C_t` = relevant context at time `t`;
- `T_acc,t` = set/structure of transformations admissible from `(S_t,C_t)` under explicitly declared accessibility rules;
- `T_real,t` = transformation actually realized at `t`;
- `S_(t+1)` = resulting state;
- `H_t` = trajectory prefix through time `t`.

Define the accessible transformation space as:
`T_acc,t = A(S_t,C_t,R_t)`
where `A` is an independently specified accessibility mapping and `R_t` denotes declared rules/constraints relevant to admissibility.

`T_acc` must not be defined from `T_real`, future outcomes, or value.

## 4. Transformation-space dynamics
The candidate object is the time evolution:
`T_acc,t → T_acc,t+1`.

A general transition is:
`(S_t,C_t,T_acc,t,T_real,t) → (S_(t+1),C_(t+1),T_acc,t+1)`.

This is a descriptive formalism, not a causal assertion.

## 5. Accessibility-space change
Define a domain-appropriate difference operator:
`ΔT_acc,t = D(T_acc,t,T_acc,t+1)`.

`D` must be declared before analysis and must distinguish, where applicable, additions, losses, substitutions, or structural changes in accessible transformations.

No universal metric is assumed. Different domains may require different representations of transformation spaces, but the semantic role of `T_acc` must remain invariant.

## 6. Accessibility equivalence
Two cases are accessibility-equivalent at `t` if their declared transformation spaces are equivalent under the chosen representation equivalence relation:
`T_acc,A,t ≡_T T_acc,B,t`.

The equivalence relation must be defined independently of realized outcomes.

Equality of labels is insufficient; equivalence must be based on transformation semantics relevant to the domain.

## 7. Realization relation
`T_real,t ∈ T_acc,t` is required for an admissible realization.

The formalism permits:
`T_acc,A,t ≡_T T_acc,B,t`
while
`T_real,A,t ≠ T_real,B,t`.

TR-131 supplies one finite witness of this pattern under its frozen construction.

## 8. Trajectory
A trajectory is:
`H_n = (S_0,T_real,0,S_1,T_real,1,...,T_real,n-1,S_n)`.

The trajectory is generated by realized transformations and resulting state transitions.

`T_acc` is not itself the trajectory.

## 9. Feedback hypothesis
A central research hypothesis is that realized transformations and resulting states can alter future accessibility:
`(S_t,T_real,t,C_t) → T_acc,t+1`.

This must remain an empirical hypothesis. It cannot be assumed merely because `T_acc` is state-dependent.

## 10. Separation of levels
The formalism distinguishes:
1. accessibility: what can be transformed;
2. realization: what transformation occurs;
3. trajectory: what sequence of states and transformations follows;
4. outcome: domain-specific consequence;
5. value: evaluated consequence.

No level is defined by a later level.

## 11. Domain-independent requirements
For cross-domain use, an implementation must specify:
- transformation identity;
- admissibility/preconditions;
- state representation;
- context representation;
- accessibility mapping;
- realization event;
- successor-state mapping;
- trajectory representation;
- accessibility-space comparison;
- outcome and value functions, if used.

Only the semantic roles are required to remain invariant. Encoding and domain-specific state variables may differ.

## 12. Comparison with existing formalisms
The basic state/action/transition/trajectory machinery is not claimed as novel. Planning and action formalisms already represent feasible actions, successor states and action sequences. Affordance formalisms already represent action possibilities and their activation/actualization. ## 13. Candidate distinctive property
The specific proposition to test is whether `T_acc,t` can serve as a common, explicit, time-indexed analytical object whose evolution can be compared across materially different adaptive systems, while maintaining a formal distinction from realization and trajectory.

This is a hypothesis about analytical utility, not a novelty claim.

## 14. Required evidence of contribution
A TGCV contribution would require demonstration that the formalism provides at least one of:
1. a cross-domain representation of transformation-space change using invariant semantics;
2. explanatory distinctions under equivalent `T_acc` that ordinary transition representations do not expose;
3. measurable trajectory consequences conditional on changes in `T_acc`;
4. systematic feedback from realized trajectories to future `T_acc`;
5. a useful value analysis that does not collapse the mechanism into the outcome.

## 15. Falsification conditions
The candidate formalism is weakened or rejected if:
- `T_acc` cannot be operationalized independently of realization;
- transformation-space changes are only relabelled state changes with no additional explanatory content;
- every proposed cross-domain result requires domain-specific semantics that destroy comparability;
- ordinary transition/planning models reproduce all claimed explanatory distinctions without loss;
- observed value relations cannot be separated from outcome or mechanism effects.

## 16. Causal boundary
The formalism does not assume:
`ΔT_acc → ΔValue`.

Possible causal pathways remain open and must be tested separately:
`ΔT_acc → ΔT_acc,future`;
`ΔT_acc → T_real`;
`T_real → trajectory`;
`trajectory → outcome`;
`outcome → value`.

These arrows are hypotheses, not definitions.

## 17. Relation to THC / transformational intelligence
THC remains a provisional explanatory label and is not required by this formalism.

Transformational intelligence remains non-canonical.

The formalism should be tested independently of either label.
