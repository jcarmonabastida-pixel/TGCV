## 1. Purpose
Compare the provisional TGCV construct Transformation-Handling Capacity (THC) against adjacent established constructs before any claim of novelty or new experimentation.

## 2. Evidence basis
Dynamic capabilities explicitly encompass sensing, seizing and transforming, including processes associated with strategic change, resource reconfiguration and altered organizational trajectories. See the cited literature in the accompanying research record.

Affordance research already distinguishes possibilities for action from their perception and actualization, and studies mechanisms and conditions through which affordances become actualized and produce outcomes.

Self-adaptive systems research already studies adaptation logic and realization through feedback/control mechanisms such as MAPE-K.

Consequently, neither capability to transform nor selection/actualization of possibilities is sufficient by itself to establish a novel TGCV construct.

## 4. Potentially distinctive TGCV element
The strongest candidate distinction is not ability to transform.

It is the explicit analytical separation:

T_acc = accessible transformation space

from:

THC = system capacity/mechanism for handling that space

with:

THC → realization/selection → trajectory

and value treated as a downstream construct rather than the definition of THC.

This distinction is potentially useful because it creates a common analytical vocabulary across domains. However, it is not yet demonstrated to be theoretically novel.

## 5. Main novelty risk
Affordance actualization already separates possible action from actualization and examines mechanisms through which possibilities become action and outcomes.

Dynamic capabilities already address sensing, seizing and transforming and can explain strategic trajectory changes.

Self-adaptive systems already formalize adaptation decisions and execution.

Therefore the burden for TGCV is to demonstrate that T_acc is a materially different analytical object from affordances/action possibilities and that THC is not simply a relabelling of actualization, dynamic capability, adaptation logic, search, or learning.

## 6. Strong candidate for TGCV differentiation
A potentially stronger claim would be:

TGCV proposes a cross-domain analytical decomposition in which a formally characterized space of accessible transformations is separated from the mechanisms/capacities that select and realize transformations, with subsequent trajectories treated as endogenous consequences and value treated as a separate downstream relation.

This is a candidate research contribution, not yet an established novelty claim.

## 7. Required tests of distinctiveness
Before creating a new experiment, TGCV should test whether the proposed decomposition provides at least one of the following beyond existing constructs:
1. a common representation applicable across materially different adaptive domains;
2. a formal distinction between accessibility and realization that existing constructs do not preserve;
3. measurable variables for transformation handling that are not reducible to the accessible transformation space;
4. trajectory-level predictions conditional on equivalent accessible transformation spaces;
5. a value analysis that does not define the mechanism through its outcome.

If none is demonstrated, TGCV should integrate with existing theory rather than create THC as a new construct.

## 8. Terminology decision
Retain THC — Transformation-Handling Capacity — only as a temporary internal label.

Do not use Transformational Intelligence as a canonical TGCV construct at this stage.

Do not claim that THC is a new capability class.

## 9. Research implication
TR-131 now has a more useful role: it supplies a concrete demonstration that the tested representation (S,C,T_acc) does not by itself determine realization.

The next research question is therefore not whether Pi can be proved irreducible.

It is whether a transformation-handling construct can be independently defined and shown to explain realization and trajectory differences conditional on equivalent accessible transformation spaces.
