# TGCV — Experimental Lines v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Status:** `CURRENT — EXPERIMENTAL ARCHITECTURE`

## Purpose

The TGCV empirical programme evaluates two logically independent questions:

1. **Explanatory/discriminatory capacity:** whether TGCV explains, discriminates or reconstructs relevant transformation/accessibility structure better than appropriate alternatives.
2. **Practical utility:** whether using TGCV produces measurable improvements in decisions, actions, operational outcomes or value construction.

**Neither line is a prerequisite for the other.**

## Line E — Explanatory / Discriminatory

Tests may address:

- explanatory adequacy;
- discriminatory power;
- state sufficiency and transformational-space irreducibility;
- reconstruction;
- cross-domain consistency;
- trajectory/accessibility representation;
- falsifiable differentiation from competing representations.

A positive result supports the corresponding explanatory/discriminatory claim only within its tested scope.

## Line U — Practical Utility

The utility line progresses through evidence layers where appropriate:

### U1 — Decision-space utility

Does TGCV expose decision-relevant alternatives, constraints or dependencies not exposed by the baseline at the same decision point?

### U2 — Decision-performance utility

Does TGCV-assisted decision-making improve decision quality, completeness or efficiency relative to the baseline?

### U3 — Action/outcome utility

Does a TGCV-informed action produce better downstream operational outcomes than the conventional approach under controlled comparison?

### U4 — Value-construction utility

Does TGCV use produce measurable economic, operational or strategic value in a defined practical context?

Each layer requires its own ex-ante operationalisation and must not inherit a positive conclusion from the preceding layer.

## Independence rule

The programme must never implement the following implicit gate:

`EXPLANATORY_SUPERIORITY = PASS -> UTILITY_TEST_ALLOWED`

Instead:

`EXPLANATORY_LINE` and `UTILITY_LINE` are parallel empirical lines.

Results may subsequently be combined analytically to determine whether explanatory/discriminatory properties predict or accompany practical utility, but such a relationship is itself an empirical question.

## Interpretation matrix

| Explanatory/discriminatory result | Utility result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Positive | Strong combined evidence within tested scope |
| Positive | Negative | Conceptually/analytically useful but practically limited |
| Negative | Positive | Potentially useful methodology despite weak superiority claim |
| Negative | Negative | Evidence against current TGCV formulation and/or utility proposition |

These are result classes, not predetermined outcomes.

## Fixture versus industrial evidence

Class-II fixtures can test bounded methodological utility and decision/action mechanisms. They cannot automatically establish industrial utility, production benefit, financial value or generalisation.

Industrial utility requires an appropriate industrial evidence boundary and a valid comparator.

## Scientific consequence

A TGCV utility test is therefore a first-class empirical test of the programme, not a downstream reward granted only after theoretical superiority has been established.

This preserves the possibility that TGCV's principal contribution could ultimately be:

- explanatory;
- discriminatory;
- practically methodological;
- value-constructive;
- or some empirically supported combination of these.
