## Purpose

This note presents the formulation of an independent doctoral research programme developed for an application to IE University’s PhD in Complex Systems for Societal and Technological Transitions. The programme was provisionally named the Theory of Construction of Value of Generative Systems (TGCV).

## Scientific problem

Can interactions that modify the set of transformations accessible to a system be represented through a minimal, domain-independent analytical framework, and can that representation add explanatory value beyond existing domain-specific accounts?

## Why telecommunications matters

Telecommunications offers a rich context because networks evolve through software, AI and programmability, with fixed, mobile and satellite capabilities interacting alongside cloud, cybersecurity, data, platforms and digital services. Interactions among infrastructure, software, data, AI, organisational capabilities, partners and ecosystem relationships may alter what becomes feasible afterwards.

## Orange as strategic context

Orange was presented as a strategically relevant context for discussing this scientific problem, particularly around next-generation networks, AI deployment, trusted cloud and AI services, cybersecurity, digital services and shared innovation capabilities.

This relevance is contextual and scientific. Orange was not proposed as a validation authority or predefined case study.

## Research architecture at application time

The application note described five elements:

1. System and state
2. Accessible transformation repertoire
3. Interaction
4. Modification of transformation space
5. Value implications

This is preserved as application history. The current canonical Core is defined independently in `01_CORE/`.
