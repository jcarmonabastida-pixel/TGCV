## Analytical construction found

The source develops a phenomenological equation for the Theory of the Adjacent Possible (TAP). It studies a system in which combinatorial innovation causes the number of possible states/objects to expand, with several implementations and analytic/numerical solutions. The authors explicitly connect TAP to economics, social science, environmental change and evolutionary systems. citeturn1academia33turn1academia30

The important prior-art feature for TGCV is that **the possibility/configuration space itself is treated as dynamically growing as a consequence of combinatorial innovation**. In the biocosmology formulation, the state-space expansion is not merely an observed outcome; it is the modeled phenomenon.

## Absorption classification

**AC2 — VERY STRONG / NEAR-DIRECT STRUCTURAL ANTECEDENT**  
**AC3 — NOT ESTABLISHED**

This source substantially strengthens the prior-art boundary around the claim that a system's future possibility space can grow dynamically through mechanisms that combine existing elements. It is particularly relevant because the expansion is formalized mathematically rather than treated only conceptually.

Nevertheless, TAP's primitive is the expanding **space of possible states/objects**, whereas TGCV's remaining candidate is an explicit space of **accessible transformations** and its change `ΔT_acc`, with accessibility defined through a general predicate and connected downstream to reachability, trajectory, outcome and value.

## Decision

Retain as a major AC2 prior-art boundary. It does not establish AC3 architectural absorption.
