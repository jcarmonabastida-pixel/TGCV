# TGCV — Reachability Link / Trajectory Sufficiency Gate v0.1

**Status:** RECONSTRUCTED / WORKING  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Precondition:** Dynamic ΔT_acc Test = PASS — dynamic distinction formally specified; empirical validation remains open.

## 1. Purpose

Test whether a change in accessible transformations can be analytically linked to a change in reachable futures and trajectory structure, while preserving the distinction between:

`ΔT_acc`, `ΔReach`, and `ΔTrajectory`.

The gate asks whether these are sequentially related analytical objects rather than different labels for the same object.

This is a formal/conceptual gate. It does not establish causal sufficiency or predictive superiority.

## 2. Definitions

Let:

`T_acc,t = {τ ∈ U_τ | P_τ(S_t,C_t,L)=1}`.

Let `Reach_t` denote the set of futures/states/configurations reachable from the current state under the transformations accessible at time `t` and under the fixed transition semantics.

Let `Trajectory_t` denote the set or structure of admissible temporal paths generated by successive accessible transformations.

The intended analytical ordering is:

`T_acc,t → Reach_t → Trajectory_t`

and, for changes:

`ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory`.

The arrows mean **analytical dependency/constraint**, not automatically causation.

## 3. Minimal formal relation

For a fixed state/context and transition semantics, changing the available transformation membership can alter which immediate transitions are available. Those changes can alter the closure of reachable futures, which can alter the admissible trajectory structure.

A useful representation is:

`Reach(S_t,T_acc,t) = {s' | ∃ finite sequence (τ_1,...,τ_n), τ_i ∈ T_acc along the sequence, and Exec(τ_1,...,τ_n) reaches s'}`.

Then:

`T_acc,t ≠ T_acc,t+1`

can imply:

`Reach_t ≠ Reach_t+1`

in non-degenerate cases, but the implication is **not universal**. A changed transformation may be redundant with another transformation or may not affect reachable futures under the relevant constraints.

Similarly:

`Reach_t ≠ Reach_t+1`

can produce a different trajectory structure, but not every reachability difference necessarily changes every trajectory measure.

Therefore the gate tests existence of informative cases, not universal implication.

## 4. Canonical counterfactual constructions

### R-T1 — ΔT_acc without ΔReach

Two systems have different accessible transformation sets, but the newly gained/lost transformation is reachable-equivalent to an existing alternative under the fixed semantics.

Result:

`ΔT_acc ≠ 0`, `ΔReach = 0`.

Purpose: prevent overclaiming that every accessibility change must change reachable futures.

### R-T2 — ΔT_acc with ΔReach

A transformation becomes accessible and enables a future state/configuration that no previously accessible sequence could reach.

Result:

`ΔT_acc ≠ 0`, `ΔReach ≠ 0`.

Purpose: establish the non-degenerate analytical link.

### R-T3 — Same Reach, different Trajectory structure

Two systems expose the same reachable endpoints but differ in admissible path structure, path length, ordering constraints, or intermediate states.

Result:

`ΔReach = 0`, `ΔTrajectory ≠ 0`.

Purpose: show that reachability is not sufficient to characterize trajectory structure.

### R-T4 — Same observed trajectory, different counterfactual trajectory set

The realized path is identical, while the set of alternative admissible continuations differs because `T_acc` differs.

Result:

`ObservedTrajectory_t = ObservedTrajectory'_t`, but

`TrajectorySet_t ≠ TrajectorySet'_t`.

Purpose: preserve counterfactual information beyond observed execution.

### R-T5 — ΔReach without identical ΔT_acc

Two systems may differ in reachable futures for reasons other than transformation-accessibility membership, such as changed constraints or initial conditions.

Purpose: prevent collapsing Reach into T_acc and establish that the relationship is conditional rather than an identity.

## 5. Sufficiency test

The central question is:

> Does `Reach` contain all information represented by `T_acc`, or does it discard information about available immediate transformations and alternatives?

`Reach` is generally a consequence/closure over available transformations. Multiple distinct `T_acc` sets can generate the same reachable set.

Therefore:

`T_acc ≠ Reach` as analytical objects.

Likewise, `Trajectory` contains temporal path information that can distinguish systems with identical endpoint reachability.

Therefore:

`Reach ≠ Trajectory` as analytical objects.

The intended architecture preserves the distinctions:

`T_acc` = what transformations are accessible now;

`Reach` = what futures are reachable through admissible sequences;

`Trajectory` = how those futures can be traversed over time.

## 6. Domain-level reconstruction

### MDE / transformation systems

Applicable transformation rules/operators determine immediate alternatives. Their closure under successive application determines reachable models, while rule sequences determine transformation trajectories.

The three objects are therefore separable even where native tooling represents them through one transition system.

### Self-adaptive systems

The adaptation space determines currently selectable adaptations. Successive adaptation choices determine reachable configurations and adaptation trajectories.

An adaptation-space change can matter downstream, but not every option change must change the reachable configuration set.

### Organizational capability/opportunity systems

Capability/opportunity structures can constrain which capability-changing moves are feasible. The set of feasible moves, the opportunities reachable after sequences of moves, and the resulting path dependence are analytically distinguishable.

The mapping is bounded and does not assert that organizational literature already contains TGCV's full architecture.

### State-space / reachability systems

The transition relation determines immediate possible moves; its transitive closure determines reachable states; paths through the relation determine trajectories.

This is the strongest prior-art pressure against claiming the downstream relation itself as novel.

TGCV's candidate distinction therefore cannot be “reachability exists” or “trajectories depend on transitions.” Those are absorbed prior art. The residual candidate is the explicit transversal representation of **accessible transformations and their change** as the upstream analytical object.

### Generativity / adjacent possible

Changes in possible future objects can alter reachable novelty spaces and future trajectories. However, possible objects/outcomes are not identical to the transformations that make them accessible.

This preserves a bounded distinction between possibility-space expansion and transformation-accessibility change.

## 7. Falsifiers

The candidate architecture should be rejected or materially weakened if any of the following is demonstrated:

**F1. Reachability identity:** `Reach` is sufficient to reconstruct `T_acc` and `ΔT_acc` with no information loss relevant to the research question.

**F2. Trajectory identity:** trajectory representation fully subsumes both accessible transformation membership and its change.

**F3. No informative downstream case:** no admissible system can exhibit `ΔT_acc ≠ 0` together with a consequential `ΔReach ≠ 0` under fixed semantics.

**F4. Universal redundancy:** whenever `ΔT_acc` changes, the only information added is already encoded by native reachability/trajectory representations.

**F5. Cross-domain collapse:** correct reconstruction across the five selected domains shows that `T_acc` is merely a renamed instance of one common prior construct.

**F6. Hidden circularity:** Reach or Trajectory must be used to define present accessibility, making `T_acc` dependent on downstream outcomes.

## 9. Gate decision

**PASS — BOUNDED REACHABILITY / TRAJECTORY SUFFICIENCY DISTINCTION ESTABLISHED.**

The gate establishes that, at the analytical level, `T_acc`, `Reach`, and `Trajectory` are not generally interchangeable:

`T_acc` captures current accessible transformation alternatives;

`Reach` captures futures obtainable through sequences of admissible transformations;

`Trajectory` captures temporal/path structure.

There are valid non-degenerate constructions in which a change in `T_acc` changes reachable futures, while other constructions show that not every `ΔT_acc` must change `Reach`.

Consequently the architectural chain

`ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory`

is defensible as a **conditional analytical relation**, not as a universal causal law.

## 10. Scientific boundary

This gate does not establish:

- empirical causal effect;
- universal applicability;
- predictive superiority;
- value creation;
- universal novelty;
- absence of prior-art equivalents.

The downstream relation is already strongly represented in prior literature. The contribution candidate remains upstream: explicit transversal representation of accessible transformations and, principally, their change `ΔT_acc`.
