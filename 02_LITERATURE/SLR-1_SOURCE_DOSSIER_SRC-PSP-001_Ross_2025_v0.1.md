# SLR-1 Source Dossier — SRC-PSP-001

**Status:** RECONSTRUCTED / WORKING
**Source:** Ross, D. (2025), *The Possibility Space Concept in Neuroscience: Possibilities, Constraints, and Explanations*, European Journal of Neuroscience. DOI: 10.1111/ejn.70038.
**Classification:** **AC2 — VERY STRONG STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE; AC3 NOT ESTABLISHED**

## Why this source is decisive

This source is materially closer to the TGCV target than the preceding affordance literature because it explicitly treats a **possibility space as a dynamic object** and states that the possibility space itself can change over time. It also distinguishes available states, accessibility, constraints, trajectories and downstream outcomes.

## Key evidence from the accessible source record

The source describes possibility-space representations as containing:

1. a total possibility space;
2. constraints operating within that space;
3. trajectories through the space.

It further states that possibility-space models can represent available but unrealized states, different degrees of accessibility/reachability, and changes in the possibility-space landscape as a system evolves. It explicitly gives cases where changes in system configuration change the repertoire of available states, and cases where changing soft constraints can introduce new repertoires or alter the accessibility of existing possibilities. citeturn0search0turn0search3

## AC2 assessment

**AC2 — VERY STRONG STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE CONFIRMED.**

The source now provides an important missing piece in the SLR-1 chain. Unlike sources that merely say that actions become applicable/inapplicable, this possibility-space framework explicitly states that:

- the repertoire of possibilities can change;
- the possibility-space landscape itself can change;
- constraints can change the accessibility of existing possibilities;
- new repertoires can appear;
- trajectories through the space represent temporal evolution;
- earlier moves can alter which downstream outcomes are available.

This is substantially closer to the TGCV proposition `T_acc,t → ΔT_acc → future reachability/trajectories` than the previous AC2 sources.

## AC3 assessment

**AC3 — NOT ESTABLISHED.**

The evidence does **not** justify classifying this source as full architectural absorption for TGCV for the following reasons.

### 1. The object is a possibility space of states, not explicitly a space of accessible transformations

The source primarily models what states a system can manifest. TGCV's `T_acc` concerns transformations/operations that are accessible from the current system conditions. The two are structurally close but not identical.

### 2. The accessibility relation is not TGCV's general transformation predicate

The source discusses constraints, accessibility and reachability, but does not formulate a general domain-independent relation of the form:

`τ ∈ T_acc iff P_τ(S,C,L)=1`.

### 3. No explicit mechanism → transformed accessibility-space architecture

Changing constraints/configuration can change the possibility space, but the source does not isolate a general explanatory mechanism `I` whose operation changes the conditions determining the accessible transformation space.

### 4. No explicit value-construction chain

The source connects possibility spaces to trajectories, adaptation and outcomes, but it does not establish the TGCV chain:

`ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value`.

### 5. No demonstrated transversal construction independent of the neuroscience domain

The possibility-space concept is presented as a general explanatory framework within neuroscience and complex-systems reasoning, but current evidence is insufficient to establish that it is already a fully domain-transversal theory matching TGCV's intended architecture.

## Falsification significance

This source **strongly falsifies** several broad TGCV novelty claims:

- that the possibility repertoire of a system has not been treated as a changing object;
- that changes in system configuration cannot be linked to changes in available possibilities;
- that accessibility/reachability and trajectories have not been represented together with a possibility-space landscape;
- that changing constraints cannot alter existing accessibility or introduce new repertoires.

Therefore, TGCV must **not** claim novelty for the generic proposition that “the space of possibilities changes over time”.

The remaining possible novelty is substantially narrower: a **transversal analytical construction of accessible transformations**, explicitly represented as a relation determined by current conditions, whose change is then propagated to future reachability, trajectories, outcomes and value.

The next operation should no longer be a broad search for “possibility spaces”. The targeted question is:

> Does prior literature already define a general **accessible transformation space** (rather than merely a state possibility space), together with an explicit rule/predicate for accessibility and a general mechanism by which system-condition changes modify that transformation space?

This is the current sharpest boundary between the known prior art and the unresolved TGCV proposition.
