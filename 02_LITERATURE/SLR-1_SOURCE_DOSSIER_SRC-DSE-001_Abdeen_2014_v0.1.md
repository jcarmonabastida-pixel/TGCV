## Retrieved full-text evidence

### E1 — explicit design-space exploration

The paper defines design-space exploration (DSE) as searching for optimal design candidates under multiple objectives and structural/numerical restrictions. Rule-based DSE seeks candidates reachable from an initial model by applying a sequence of exploration rules. citeturn2search25turn2search1

### E2 — explicit reachable-state construction

A candidate is represented by a model reached from an initial model through a sequence of rule applications. The paper therefore has an explicit initial state, transformation operators and reachable candidate states. citeturn2search25

### E3 — sequence as trajectory representation

Individuals in the optimization population are defined as sequences of rule applications leading from the initial model to a candidate model. Mutation and crossover manipulate, extend or combine those rule-execution sequences. citeturn2search25turn2search2

### E4 — feasibility/accessibility constraint

Rule applications must produce feasible solutions. Later literature describing the framework records the repair mechanism used to ensure feasible rule-application sequences, with infeasible sequences truncated or discarded when they cannot be applied to the initial model. citeturn2search5turn2search13

### E5 — constraints and objectives

Constraints are captured through model queries; objectives may be derived from models or from rule applications. The paper explicitly frames the problem as multi-objective optimization over reachable design candidates. citeturn2search25

### E6 — domain-independent abstraction

The authors explicitly characterize the proposed multi-objective rule-based DSE approach as domain independent and implemented through Eclipse-based tooling, while preserving a high-level abstraction. citeturn2search0turn2search25

## Why AC2 is justified

The source is a strong structural antecedent because it explicitly combines:

1. an initial model/state;
2. a set of transformation rules;
3. applicability/feasibility constraints;
4. reachable candidate states;
5. transformation sequences as trajectories;
6. optimization criteria over resulting candidates.

It therefore anticipates a substantial portion of TGCV's analytical relation among state, accessible transformations, reachability and trajectory. This is stronger than mere construct similarity. citeturn2search25

The source is also especially relevant because the authors describe the approach as domain independent at the level of the DSE method. citeturn2search0

## Why AC3 is NOT established

Despite its structural proximity, the source does not establish the full TGCV architecture.

### 1. The object is exploration/optimization, not change in accessibility conditions

The paper asks how to search a rule-based design space efficiently and optimize candidate designs. It does not make the transition from one accessibility structure to another — `T_acc,t ≄ T_acc,t+1` — the central analytical phenomenon.

### 2. Accessibility remains tied to a fixed rule system

The accessible candidates are generated by applying exploration rules to an initial model. The evidence does not establish a general function in which changing system conditions themselves alter the set of admissible transformations.

### 3. No explicit separation of mechanism and induced transformation-space change

Rules are both transformation operators and components of the exploration procedure. The source does not isolate a transversal explanatory mechanism whose effect is to modify the conditions determining future accessibility.

### 4. The optimization objective is not TGCV's value-construction architecture

The source optimizes design candidates against explicit criteria. This is an outcome-selection/optimization structure, not evidence for the TGCV chain `ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value` as a general value-construction theory.

## Provisional absorption assessment

**AC2 — STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE CANDIDATE.**

Abdeen et al. establishes a mature prior-art construction containing state/model, transformation rules, feasibility, reachability, trajectories and objective-based selection.

**AC3 — ARCHITECTURAL ABSORPTION: NOT ESTABLISHED.**

The unresolved TGCV-specific architectural element remains the *change in the space of accessible transformations as a consequence of changed system conditions*, rather than exploration/optimization inside a transformation space defined by an existing rule system.
