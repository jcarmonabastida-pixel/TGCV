**Source:** Thomas Brenner & Sonja zu Jeddeloh (2024), *Path dependence in an evolving system: a modeling perspective*, Cliometrica 18, 1–36. DOI `10.1007/s11698-023-00266-z`.

## Family

**State-space and reachability approaches**  
**Status:** INCLUDED — substantive comparison

## Analytical construction found

The paper explicitly distinguishes systems with a stable state space from systems in which the **set of possible states itself evolves**, with possible states being added or removed. It argues that such systems are automatically non-ergodic and develops a graphical/mathematical representation of path dependence in evolving state spaces. The source therefore provides direct prior art for changing possibility structures and their consequences for future paths. citeturn3search2

## Absorption classification

**AC2 — VERY STRONG / DIRECT ANALOGUE**  
**AC3 — NOT ESTABLISHED**

This source materially weakens any claim that TGCV is the first theory to make the evolution of a possibility/state space itself analytically important. It also strengthens the prior-art boundary around changing possibility structures affecting future trajectories.

The remaining TGCV distinction is important: the source's evolving object is a **state space**, whereas TGCV proposes an analytical object consisting of **accessible transformations** and explicitly studies its change before downstream reachability and trajectory.

## Decision

Retain as a major prior-art boundary. It contributes to the cumulative AC2 evidence but does not establish full architectural absorption.
