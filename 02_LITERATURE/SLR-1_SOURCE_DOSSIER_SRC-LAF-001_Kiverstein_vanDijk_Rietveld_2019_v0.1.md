# SLR-1 Source Dossier — SRC-LAF-001

**Status:** RECONSTRUCTED / WORKING
**Source:** Kiverstein, J., van Dijk, L., & Rietveld, E. (2019/2021), *The field and landscape of affordances: Koffka's two environments revisited*, Synthese 198, 2279–2296. DOI: 10.1007/s11229-019-02123-x.
**Classification:** **AC2 — STRONG STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE; AC3 NOT ESTABLISHED**

## Why this source was selected

This is the direct next-step candidate after SRC-DAF-001 and SRC-SCI-001 because it treats not merely individual affordances but a **landscape of affordances** and a dynamically selected **field of relevant affordances**. The paper explicitly frames the relation between landscape and field as temporally extended and reciprocal.

## Key evidence

### E1 — landscape as a structured totality of possibilities

The landscape of affordances is described as the rich set of affordances available within a form of life/ecological niche. Related work characterizes it as a nested, interrelated structure rather than a list of isolated possibilities.

### E2 — field as a context-dependent subset

The field of affordances consists of those affordances that become relevant to a particular individual in a particular situation. It is therefore a context-sensitive selection from a broader landscape.

### E3 — temporal development

The paper explicitly treats engagement with the landscape as an unfolding temporally extended process. The landscape makes possibilities available through a history of engagement, while acting on relevant affordances can further determine the possibilities available in the future.

### E4 — reciprocal change

This is the strongest TGCV-relevant evidence. The authors describe a reciprocal temporal relation: on shorter timescales the field depends on the landscape; over longer timescales the landscape depends on the field because engagement in practices maintains, continues, or further determines the possibilities the landscape offers going forward.

### E5 — possibilities are not causally fixed

The source explicitly rejects a simple causal-deterministic picture. The landscape does not causally determine individual action; possibilities are shaped through developmental history, skills, practices, and engagement.

## AC2 assessment

**AC2 — STRONG STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE CONFIRMED.**

This source goes beyond the previous candidates in one important respect: the **landscape itself** is an object of analysis, not merely the applicability of individual actions. The source also describes how engagement can further determine the possibilities available in the future.

Consequently, it is legitimate to map:

`current landscape → engagement/practice → further determination of future possibilities`.

This materially narrows TGCV's possible originality claim.

## AC3 assessment

**AC3 — NOT ESTABLISHED.**

Despite the strong proximity, the full TGCV architecture is not absorbed on current evidence.

### 1. No explicit `ΔT_acc` representation

The paper discusses development and further determination of possibilities, but does not introduce a general formal object representing the difference between two accessible-transformation spaces.

### 2. No transversal transformation-space predicate

Affordances are relational properties between environmental aspects and abilities available in a form of life. The framework is not presented as a domain-independent predicate of the form:

`τ ∈ T_acc iff P_τ(S,C,L)=1`.

### 3. No explicit reachability/trajectory chain in TGCV's sense

Temporal engagement and developmental paths are central, but the source does not formulate a general chain equivalent to:

`ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory → Outcome → Value`.

### 4. No separate mechanism layer

Reciprocal engagement and practice are explanatory components, but there is no independently specified mechanism variable whose effect is analytically separated from state/context change.

### 5. Value architecture differs

The source concerns situated normativity, relevance, solicitations and practices, not a transversal construction of value from changes in future transformation accessibility.

## Falsification significance

This source **does falsify** the following broad claims for TGCV:

- that prior theory lacks any concept of a landscape/set of interrelated action possibilities;
- that possibilities cannot be understood as temporally developing;
- that engagement with a system/environment cannot itself contribute to determining future possibilities;
- that accessibility landscapes are necessarily static.

It does **not yet falsify** the narrower proposition that a domain-independent analytical layer can explicitly represent:

`T_acc,t`, `T_acc,t+1`, and `ΔT_acc = T_acc,t+1 \ T_acc,t` / corresponding contraction/reconfiguration,

and then relate that change systematically to future reachability, trajectories, outcomes and value.

## Important methodological caution

This candidate is much closer to AC3 than the MDE cluster and should therefore trigger **citation-chain mining rather than another generic affordance search**. The immediate question is whether the field/landscape tradition has a formal successor that operationalizes the landscape as a changing state-space or possibility-space object, or explicitly measures changes in that landscape.
