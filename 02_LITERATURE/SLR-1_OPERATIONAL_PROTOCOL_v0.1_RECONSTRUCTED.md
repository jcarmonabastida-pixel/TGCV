# TGCV — SLR-1 Operational Protocol v0.1

**Status:** RECONSTRUCTED / WORKING
**Type:** Normative operational reconstruction; not a recovered historical original.
**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Gate:** SLR-1 — Architectural Originality / Prior-Art Falsification

## 1. Purpose

SLR-1 is the systematic literature gate whose purpose is to attempt to falsify the claim that the current TGCV architecture provides an original analytical construction.

SLR-1 is not designed to confirm TGCV originality. A positive originality conclusion is admissible only after systematic attempts to identify an equivalent or absorbing prior construction have been completed under this protocol.

## 2. Current TGCV boundary under test

SLR-1 does not reopen the conceptual tests already closed.

Working architectural boundary:

`Core_ontological = S`

`T_acc = F(S,C,L)`

`T_acc,t ≄ T_acc,t+1`

`ΔT_acc → ΔReach → ΔTrajectory`

`Trajectory → Outcome → Value`

`I` is an explanatory mechanism, not a Core primitive.

The object of prior-art comparison is therefore the analytical architecture centered on the explicit representation of changes in the accessible transformation structure, not merely the terminology used to describe systems, change, adaptation, capability, affordance, evolution or value.

## 3. Primary SLR-1 question

**Does the prior literature contain a construction that is structurally or architecturally equivalent to TGCV's current analytical architecture, such that TGCV's distinctive contribution is fully or materially absorbed by an existing theory/framework?**

## 4. Secondary questions

1. Does prior literature explicitly represent a system's accessible transformation space or transformational capacity as an analytical object?
2. Does prior literature explicitly analyze change in that space across states or contexts?
3. Does prior literature represent accessibility through explicit predicates/constraints rather than only observed execution?
4. Does prior literature connect changes in accessible transformations to reachable states, trajectories or subsequent outcomes?
5. Does an existing framework contain the same relation among state, accessible transformations, their change and subsequent trajectory without requiring a substantive new construct?
6. If similarities exist, are they terminological, construct-level, structural, or architectural?

## 5. Unit of comparison

The unit of comparison is the **analytical construction**, not the article title, terminology, isolated variable or application domain.

For each candidate source, comparison must identify at minimum:

- system/state representation;
- transformation representation;
- accessibility condition;
- whether accessibility is distinguished from execution;
- representation of the transformation space;
- representation of change in that space;
- relation to reachability/trajectory;
- role of mechanism/interactions;
- relation to outcomes/value, when present;
- level/granularity assumptions;
- explicitness of the architecture.

## 6. Absorption hierarchy

Every relevant source must be classified using the following ordered hierarchy.

### Level 0 — Terminological similarity

Shared words or near-synonyms without demonstrated equivalence of analytical object or relations.

**Effect:** no absorption.

### Level 1 — Construct similarity

A source contains a construct substantially resembling `T_acc`, transformational capacity, future possibilities, affordances, capability sets, option spaces, etc., but does not establish the TGCV architecture.

**Effect:** prior art; no architectural absorption by itself.

### Level 2 — Structural equivalence

A source represents substantially equivalent components and relations, including an explicit transformation-accessibility structure and its change, but architectural scope or theoretical role remains materially different.

**Effect:** strong prior-art challenge; requires explicit comparative analysis.

### Level 3 — Architectural absorption

A source/framework provides an equivalent analytical architecture in which the TGCV distinctive object and relations can be mapped without a substantive theoretical addition.

**Effect:** SLR-1 falsification condition satisfied for architectural originality.

## 7. Inclusion criteria

A source is eligible for substantive SLR-1 comparison if it satisfies at least one of the following:

- explicitly models possible/accesssible actions, transformations, capabilities, affordances or option spaces;
- explicitly distinguishes possible transformations from executed transformations;
- models how future possibilities/capabilities change as system state or conditions change;
- provides a formal or semi-formal structure comparable to `T_acc`;
- provides a theory/framework that plausibly subsumes the TGCV state → accessible-transformations → transformation-space-change → trajectory chain;
- provides a cross-domain abstraction whose architecture can plausibly be compared to TGCV.

Sources may be retained as contextual evidence even when they fail substantive inclusion, but they must not be used as Level 2/3 absorption evidence without satisfying the comparison requirements.

## 8. Exclusion criteria

Exclude from substantive absorption analysis when a source is only:

- terminologically similar without analytical correspondence;
- an application/example with no relevant general construction;
- concerned only with executed actions/events while lacking accessibility/future-possibility representation;
- concerned only with static capability inventories without change in accessibility conditions;
- concerned only with state transition without an explicit or reconstructable transformation-space object;
- concerned only with outcome/value prediction without the transformation-accessibility architecture;
- a duplicate publication or non-substantive version of another included source.

Exclusion does not erase provenance; the screening decision must be recorded.

## 9. Search strategy — normative minimum

Search must proceed in concept families rather than a single TGCV-specific query.

### Family A — transformation/accessibility

Examples of search concepts:

- accessible transformations;
- possible transformations;
- transformation space;
- space of possible actions;
- action possibilities;
- reachable transformations;
- feasible transformations;
- capability space;
- option space.

### Family B — state-dependent future possibilities

Examples:

- state-dependent capabilities;
- changing action possibilities;
- evolution of capability sets;
- changing affordance structures;
- future possibility space;
- state transition and possible actions;
- reconfiguration of capabilities/options.

### Family C — structural/dynamic change

Examples:

- transformation-space change;
- capability-set change;
- affordance change;
- reconfiguration of possible actions;
- expansion/contraction of possibilities;
- changing reachable states/trajectories.

### Family D — adjacent theories

Search must also cover established neighbouring literatures, including where relevant:

- affordance theory;
- dynamic capabilities;
- adaptive systems;
- self-* systems;
- reconfigurable systems;
- evolutionary/evolving systems;
- runtime composition;
- organizational capability change;
- state-space and reachability approaches;
- generativity and combinatorial possibility approaches.

The purpose of adjacent-family searches is falsification, not confirmation of a preselected narrative.

## 10. Source recording

Every substantive candidate must receive a source dossier containing:

1. bibliographic identity;
2. stable source identifier/URL where available;
3. search query/family that retrieved it;
4. inclusion/exclusion decision;
5. exact location of relevant evidence;
6. extracted factual claims;
7. analytical interpretation, kept separate from facts;
8. TGCV component mapping;
9. absorption level (0–3);
10. rationale for the level;
11. unresolved ambiguities;
12. reviewer/date/version metadata.

## 11. Evidence versus fact discipline

The SLR subsystem must maintain three distinct layers:

`Source → Evidence → Fact → Interpretation/Absorption decision`

A fact must describe what the source actually establishes. An absorption judgement is an analytical inference and must never be stored as though it were a source fact.

## 12. Falsification criterion

SLR-1 is considered to have falsified TGCV architectural originality if at least one sufficiently documented Level-3 source is identified, or if a convergent body of Level-2 evidence demonstrates that the allegedly distinctive architecture is already materially established by prior theory and TGCV adds no substantive analytical relation or object.

A Level-0 or Level-1 match cannot falsify architectural originality.

A Level-2 match alone does not automatically falsify; it triggers explicit comparative review.

## 13. Positive result criterion

SLR-1 may support a provisional **NO ARCHITECTURAL ABSORPTION IDENTIFIED** conclusion only when:

- the predefined concept families have been searched;
- adjacent literatures relevant to the architecture have been screened;
- candidate sources have been documented;
- plausible Level-2/3 candidates have received substantive comparison;
- no Level-3 absorption has been established;
- unresolved Level-2 cases are explicitly recorded.

This is a bounded literature finding, not proof that no prior art exists anywhere.

## 14. Core-change rule

SLR-1 cannot modify the TGCV Core automatically.

Any evidence capable of challenging the current architecture must be escalated through an explicit architecture/gate decision with provenance, comparative analysis and versioned decision record.

## 15. Execution boundary

This protocol authorizes **protocol preparation and subsequent systematic literature work** only.

It does not authorize:

- new EXT-1.1 empirical execution;
- modification of the frozen Rust protocol;
- post-hoc changes to EXT-1.1;
- inferential analysis of EXT-1.1;
- alteration of closed TR-131–TR-140 decisions.

## 16. Stopping rule

The first Level-3 candidate must not terminate the entire audit. It terminates the search for purposes of establishing that at least one architectural absorption exists, but the source and comparison must still be documented sufficiently for an auditable decision.

If no Level-3 candidate is found, searching continues until the predefined concept families and relevant adjacent literatures have been screened to the extent documented in the final search log.
